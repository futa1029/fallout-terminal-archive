'''Adam''' is a lead farmer residing in the greater [[Los Angeles]] area {{In|FOTV}}.

==Background==
In 2296, Adam is scavenging for old scrap and discarded bullet casings with metal detectors. However, it is implied that he once had one or more violent encounters with [[the Ghoul]] in the past, involving a firefight.

Adam eventually settled down and had three children: his older son [[Roofus]], younger son [[Tommy (TV series)|Tommy]], and daughter [[Sandra (TV series)|Sandra]].

==''Fallout'' TV series==
===[[The Radio]]===
At some point in [[Timeline (2250-2300)#2296|2296]], Adam and his younger son Tommy go out on a scavenging trip, combing through the sands of the desert close to their house collecting salvage. When they return home, Tommy unloads their bounty into a pot to be melted down, while Adam removes his scavenging gear. The two then walk to their shack house, where Adam beckons to Sandra but does not receive a reply.

Inside the house, the two are alerted by the presence of the Ghoul as he eats a meal at their table. Unnerved, Adam again calls out to Sandra and is relieved to see her appear, serving the bounty hunter a glass of water. The Ghoul laughs at the notion that he had killed Adam's daughter and [[Cannibalism|is eating her]]. Adam requests that Sandra wait outside as the Ghoul comments on Adam's line of work as a lead farmer, claiming that some of his lead might still be in his body. After finishing his meal, the Ghoul reveals he came to them today just for information.

Adam assures the Ghoul that they will tell him anything if he only leaves them in peace. When the bounty hunter asks about his children, Adam states that he had an older son whom they have not heard from since he aligned himself with [[Lee Moldaver]]. The Ghoul asks if the name of the other son was Roofus, but Adam claims that he did not say his name.

The Ghoul then holds up an envelope and directs his attention to Tommy, indicating the two brothers were more involved that their father knew. Adam furiously asks Tommy what he did and what was in that envelope. The Ghoul unfolds a blood-splattered letter, peering through the bullet-sized hole in the middle and reveals that Roofus got Tommy involved with Moldaver's operations. From reading the letter, the Ghoul reveals that Roofus entrusted his younger brother to pay a courier for the safe transportation of [[Siggi Wilzig|a defector]] from the [[Enclave]], to be brought to Moldaver. He then reveals that Roofus met his demise at the bounty hunter's hands, before demanding to know Moldaver's location so he can visit her and leave them be. Tommy tries to apologize to his father for bringing trouble to their home, while Adam tries repeatedly to get Tommy to give up the information.

Caving into the pressure from his father, Tommy reveals that Moldaver is at the [[Griffith Observatory|observatory]]. Believing the matter settled, Adam calmly asks that the Ghoul leave them be as he said he would. However, the Ghoul directs his attention back to Tommy, questioning if he really would let the bounty hunter go seeing how he had killed his older brother. Against Adam's vehement denial and attempts to get his son to let the matter go, Tommy gives into his desire for revenge and tries to reach for a [[flintlock musket]] to attack. The Ghoul reacts faster and shoots Tommy with his [[Dom Pedro (TV series weapon)|revolver]], killing him. He then walks out as Adam mourns the loss of his second son.

==Personality==
In his interactions with the Ghoul, Adam demonstrates himself to be a dedicated family man, deeply caring for the wellbeing of his children and doing everything in his power to avoid angering the Ghoul when he comes to their home. This also extends to trying to keep them apart from the militant activities of powerful figures like Lee Moldaver.

==Notes==
While scavenging, Adam and Tommy are first shown wearing aged, crudely patched up suits of [[NCR Ranger combat armor]], which was worn by the [[NCR Veteran Ranger|Veteran Rangers]] of the [[New California Republic]].

'''Arnold''' was an intern who worked at [[Galaxy News Network]] in [[Timeline#2077|2077]]. 

==Background==
Arnold worked with the announcer of a [[Fallout - A Special LIVE Report from Galaxy News|Special LIVE report on]] [[Vault 33]], writing the script for the special. The [[Reporter (TV series)|reporter]] hosting the show became increasingly frustrated with Arnold's work, and how he kept giving him advertisements instead of news stories to read. As the reporter's frustration with Arnold grew, he questioned how Arnold had even got his job in the first place, flat out asking if he had been hired because of nepotism. Arnold told him that his uncle was the [[general manager of Galaxy News]]. He also noted that Arnold appeared to be fifteen despite really being twenty-three.

As the Special LIVE report continued, the reporter became even more frustrated by Arnold's unprofessional scripting and information gathering. The reporter continued to chide Arnold for not knowing how to do his job, as Arnold did not respond properly to situations and failed to answer the reporter's questions. The reporter became so infuriated with Arnold that he had an emotional breakdown live on air and insulted Arnold for being single while questioning his own will to live and the meaning of life.

At one point, the reporter questioned whether Arnold himself had secured a spot in a [[Vault]] and was dismayed to discover that he and Arnold had both been assigned to live in the same Vault. However, the reporter eventually warmed to the idea of spending his life in a Vault with Arnold on the condition that the Vault tour would finally begin.

==Appearances==
Arnold's muffled voice can be heard several times in ''[[Fallout - A Special LIVE Report from Galaxy News]]''.

'''Aspirants''' are characters in the [[Fallout (TV series)|''Fallout'' TV series]].

==Background==
{{main|Brotherhood of Steel hierarchy}}
In the [[Brotherhood of Steel]] chapter known as the [[Knights of San Fernando]], aspirants are low-ranking Brotherhood trainees, which seems to include both children and older adult trainees. Aside from many unnamed aspirants, they include [[Maximus]], [[Dane (TV series)|Dane]], and [[Thaddeus]] (all of whom are eventually promoted to [[squire (TV series)|squire]]), as well as [[Thaddeus' minion|Thaddeus' five minions]] and the [[young aspirant]].

==''Fallout'' TV series==
===Season 1===
====[[The End (episode)|The End]]====
The Brotherhood is introduced with a cut to [[Thaddeus' minion|several aspirants]] in white T-shirts in the desert, all beating a peer into the dirt. The victim, [[Maximus]], becomes the perspective character; when they're done with Maximus, [[Dane (TV series)|Dane]] approaches Maximus and tries to console the beaten-up Maximus. As they head off, several other aspirants are seen performing their daily duties.

Afterward, within the Brotherhood of Steel airbase hanger, [[Felix (TV series)|Cleric Felix]] teaches the studious aspirants their purpose of becoming a [[Squire (TV series)|squire]] to a [[Brotherhood_of_Steel_ranks#Knights|knight]] of the [[Brotherhood of Steel]]. He's shown to act in a physically disciplinary way when Maximus gets an answer wrong that he asked.

Just then, [[the Prydwen|the ''Prydwen'']] arrives, drawing the attention of the aspirants there, and watching as the knights in their [[T-60 power armor]] arrive. Maximus wondered why they were there and would later perform latrine duties until Dane stopped him and took him to the hangar where the T-60 power armor suits were at. Maximus was enamored by it until [[Brotherhood soldier (TV series)|officers]] [[Shortsight]] and another had business with Dane.

Early the next morning, several aspirants are seen tossing cement scraps into a makeshift basketball hoop. Inside the cabins for the aspirants, some aspirants were seen preparing themselves as Maximus woke up and saw a commotion outside made by his fellow aspirants. There, Maximus learns that Dane was one of the many anointed to squire. As Dane and his fellow aspirants follow him into a cabin, Maximus despondently watches him.

That night, Maximus overhears many of his peers talk about who will be anointed next, and has a meltdown over it.

The next day, Maximus, Thaddeus and his fellow aspirants awaken to the sound of Dane screaming in pain to learn that someone placed a razor blade in their boot. Ultimately, Maximus is blamed for the incident and is taken into questioning for it. While being led to his interrogation, some aspirants were seen performing physical combat training against each other.

[[Quintus|Elder Cleric Quintus]] interrogates Maximus over the plausibility of being a suspect for Dane's injury and, in the end, passes Dane's anointment to him and assigns him to [[Titus|Knight Titus]].

When Maximus visits Dane in the infirmary, an injured aspirant is seen lying on a cot. Dane and Maximus talk to each other, and when asked who Dane suspects injured them, they answer that they've narrowed it down to Thaddeus and his cohorts.

Eventually, some aspirants, including Thaddeus, sit in observation of Maximus' anointment ceremony.

Later, aspirants are seen in the multi-single file crowds where Elder Cleric Quintis announces to the knights and their newly appointed squires their task. It involved hunting a [[Enclave]] member with a [[Cold fusion|dangerous device]] in his possession. Paper copies of the target are passed around by Thaddeus and one of his minions.

Once the speech was over and the papers with the targets were passed around, the aspirants watched as the knights and their respective squires headed off.

They can be seen working outside the [[Area 51]] base.

====[[The Demon in the Snow]]====

'''Overseer Benjamin''', or '''Ben''', is the [[overseer]] of [[Vault 4]] in [[Timeline#2296|2296]] {{In|FOTV}}.

==Background==
Benjamin's ancestors were survivors of the [[Great War]] who were accepted into Vault 4 by its original dwellers, scientists who adopted a policy of accepting refugees from the wasteland for use as test subjects. These subjects were put under genetic experimentation, including hybridization with radiation-resistant species. For example, Benjamin's maternal great-uncle, [[Peter (TV series)|Peter]], was (allegedly) turned into a [[Gulper (TV series)|gulper]] by the experiments. The test subjects eventually managed to break free and overthrow the scientists, and they have ruled the Vault ever since, continuing the Vault's refugee policy but for truly benevolent reasons.

==''Fallout'' TV series==
===[[The Trap]]===
While Lucy and Maximus are eating, they are introduced to him by [[Birdie]]. They are disturbed by his one eye, but do not wish to offend him by saying anything. He tells them to stay out of level 12 and mentions the Vault only has one foosball table.

Later, Lucy talks to Ben in his office about toilets. He refers to her as Goosey because her name is Goosey on his data sheet. Ben claims to be open-minded and discusses how troublesome surface refugees can be with their smelly food and weird ideas. When Lucy asks about level 12, he shuts down the conversation and orders her to leave.

===[[The Radio]]===
He and Birdie handcuff Lucy when they catch her in level 12. They explain to Lucy what the Vault's experiments are about.

Later, Ben sentences Lucy to death by exile and gives her two weeks of survival supplies.

==Personality==
[[File:FOTV Overseer Benjamin.png|thumb|250px|Benjamin]]
Polite and amenable on the surface, he secretly thinks little of the [[New California Republic|NCR]] refugees who have come to populate his birthplace. In the company of another Vault Dweller, he disparages the "surfies" as smelling bad and having strange, barbaric customs.

Despite harboring this ugly prejudice, he is not known to use his power as the overseer to mistreat or ostracize the immigrant population, and he stands out in the setting as a particularly benevolent and merciful ruler. His highest priority is keeping the grotesque post-human mutants of Level 12 secure and comfortable, especially because he has family among them. When Lucy disfigures an innocent caregiver while intruding on Level 12, her punishment is to be released unharmed to the surface with two weeks' worth of supplies, a measure that Benjamin considers extreme and borderline cruel.

Some of the other Vault 4 natives wear clothing that hides their [[mutations]], but the overseer does not. He appears to be oblivious when Lucy displays alarm at his own mutation, as if unaware in the moment that his appearance and concept of normalcy may be considered confusing or off-putting to outsiders. Benjamin is overall characterized as a highly hypocritical, naïve man who is nonetheless guided by compassion and a strong concept of human rights.

'''Bert''' was a resident of [[Vault 33]] {{In|FOTV}}.

==Background==
He married [[Steph Harper]] on March 17, [[Timeline (2250-2300)#2294|2294]] and they consummated their marriage the same night, with Steph commenting that her wedding dress "got off almost as fast as Burt{{sic}} did." By 2296, they had conceived their first child.

Per Steph's description, Bert was very fond of his shoes and kept them in very good condition; he would also talk about them so much to the point that he would bore everyone around him into leaving.

==''Fallout'' TV series==
===[[The End (episode)|The End]]===
Bert is part of the group that welcomes the Vault 32 dwellers into Vault 33 on the day of [[Lucy MacLean]]'s wedding. When they see Lucy's soon-to-be husband [[Monty (TV series)|Monty]], Steph comments, "Lucky," with Bert giving her an appalled look from behind while Steph smiles, seemingly satisfied with his reaction. During the wedding reception in the atrium cornfield, Steph and Bert sit at the same table with Lucy and Monty. After [[Overseer]] [[Hank MacLean]] gives his speech, Bert and Steph are seen dancing in the background.

The Vault 32 dwellers later reveal themselves to be raiders, indiscriminately attacking the Vault 33 dwellers. Bert is caught in the chaos and is killed after his neck is sliced by one of the raiders.<!--Hard to see on screen, but the actor posted a picture of his makeup on Insta; will find later--> Steph is seen cradling his corpse before attacking the raiders, driven by grief and rage. This causes her to be stabbed in the eye with a fork, though she manages to grab a submachine gun and kill at least one raider.

'''Ranger Biff''' is a member of the [[New California Republic Rangers]] in [[Fallout TV series season 2|season two]] of the [[Fallout (TV series)|''Fallout'' TV series]].

==Background==
Ranger Biff is one of the last remaining holdouts of the [[New California Republic]] in the [[Mojave Wasteland]], having remained guarding against [[Caesar's Legion]] since the [[Fall of Shady Sands]] and their subsequent loss of communication with other NCR forces in the region. Together with [[Rodriguez|Captain Rodriguez]] and another veteran Ranger, the trio have been camped at an [[NCR outpost|outpost]] near [[Primm]] as of 2296. Biff has been passing the time by caring for a pet [[squirrel]].

Around July 2296, the group decides to pack up their outpost and leave to avoid being captured by the Legion.

==''Fallout'' TV series==
===[[The Profligate]]===
After receiving a tip from [[Victor (Securitron)|Victor]] at [[Camp Golf]] about "Rangers in the hills," the Ghoul leaves in search of the Rangers, hoping to enlist their firepower to help rescue [[Lucy MacLean]], who had been captured by the Legion. Outside Primm, he approaches the NCR outpost and is ambushed by the trio, with Biff standing to the back while Rodriguez and the other Ranger ascertain whether the Ghoul is a threat.

After the Ghoul is brought to the camp, Biff is seen with his helmet off, tending to his squirrel and muttering that they have to be ready for the rest of the NCR to return "any day now." When Rodriguez tells the Ghoul that they have been isolated for over a decade, he affirms that he believes reinforcements will come, though both Rodriguez and the Ghoul acknowledge that Biff has been left particularly disheveled by the isolation. The Ghoul tells Rodriguez that Shady Sands' destruction was a long time ago, meaning that any reinforcements they knew of from back then may no longer exist; Biff looks up forlornly as a result. He continues listening to their conversation, backing Rodriguez' claim that the NCR is winning against the Legion.

When the Ghoul refuses to help them fend off the Legion, Biff glares as he leaves, but remains seated.

===[[The Demon in the Snow]]===
Biff is seen at the NCR outpost as Lucy wakes up from her recovery. Lucy is taken aback when she first sees Biff tending to his squirrel, and when she asks whether there are any more NCR remnants, Biff adamantly maintains that there are "tons" of them, just not there at the moment. He then asks whether Lucy has been to Shady Sands, telling her that they came from there, while helping Rodriguez pack up supplies. He later thanks Lucy when she calls it a "welcome change of pace" to have met them compared to others in the wasteland. He continues to switch between packing and playing with the squirrel as Lucy talks with Rodriguez.

===[[The Other Player]]===
While travelling, Biff was abducted by [[Hank MacLean]]'s mind-controlled slaves and taken to the [[Las Vegas management Vault]], along with a [[Massive legionary|legionary]], and both were fitted with [[brain-computer interface chip]]s.

When Lucy is about to take her father to the surface to face justice, Biff and the legionary are wheeled out by the [[snake oil salesman]] and another slave, tied to desk chairs. The salesman asks Hank what to do with them since they have not been "fixed" yet, but Hank defers to Lucy to decide whether to activate the chip with the push of a button or let them free. Lucy chooses to let them free, but the legionary, who is the first of the two to be tied, immediately attempts to kill Biff. Lucy steps in to protect Biff from the legionary but is eventually overtaken. Biff attempts to keep the legionary away, only for the legionary to begin hitting him in the head with a stapler.

Lucy pleads for Hank to stop them, but Hank answering that the only way to stop them is to hit the button to activate their chips. With Biff being assaulted and no way to stop the legionary, Lucy chooses to hit the button. Biff and the legionary are near-instantly brainwashed, losing all of their previous animosity and their memories of what had happened, with the legionary helping untie Biff and leading him away to receive medical attention.

==Personality==
Biff is initially shown to be constantly on edge as a result of over a decade of combat in the Mojave. Despite this, his faith in his mission remains strong; he shares [[Rodriguez]]' belief that the NCR is still active, and that they are winning the war against the Legion, though neither claim's validity is certain.  

After being captured by Hank MacClean and implanted with a [[brain-computer interface chip]], however, he was brainwashed into becoming a model Vault-Tec employee willing to work with the captured [[Massive legionary|legionary]] he'd been fighting shortly beforehand.

'''Bill''' is a character who appears in the first episode of the second season of the [[Fallout (TV series)|''Fallout'' TV series]].

==Background==
Bill is a heavyset, bald construction worker dissatisfied with [[RobCo Industries]]' policy of replacing humans with robots.

==''Fallout'' TV series==
===[[The Innovator]]===
At a bar, he and other protesters from the [[automation riots]] watch a speech by the fake Mr. House, calling the corporation's CEO a parasite and saying he didn't vote for him. He is interrupted by the voice of a stranger (the real [[Mr. House]]), who, on the contrary, speaks highly of House's performance, claiming he has more power than any politician in [[Washington]] and agreeing with Bill's caustic comment that he is House's biggest fan. The stranger begins to discuss obsolescence, but Bill and his friends throw him out of the bar, threatening to beat him up. House demands that Bill punch him in the jaw first, which he agrees to.

Afterward, the businessman shows him the trunk of a car containing $31 million in cash, offering him a deal: Bill will keep all the money, but in exchange, he must insert an [[Brain-computer interface chip|unknown device]] into the back of his head, calling it "a little market research." Bill resists the offer, grabs House by the jacket, and threatens to break his hands, which he "never used," and take all the money. But House seizes the moment and thrusts the device into his neck, then orders him to "get rid of his friends," handing him a bat. Mesmerized, Bill beats his friends to death, and House begins to twist the device's switch, causing Bill's head to explode. After picking up the chip, Mr. House concludes that "the world may end, but technology will continue to advance."

'''Brotherhood soldiers''' are characters in the [[Fallout (TV series)|''Fallout'' TV series]].

==Background==
{{main|Brotherhood of Steel hierarchy}}
These soldiers are low-ranking active duty members of the [[Brotherhood of Steel]] chapter known as the [[Knights of San Fernando]]. Their uniform consists of grey/black combat suits with red undershirts.

Soldiers are led by [[Brotherhood officer]]s in red overcoats. [[Vertibird pilot (TV series)|Vertibird pilots]] who wear grey flight suits and brown leather flight helmets also appear alongside them.

==''Fallout'' TV series==
===[[The End (episode)|The End]]===
Some soldiers are seen standing in the crowd of [[Quintus|Elder Cleric Quintus']] speech about a [[Siggi Wilzig|target]] and his [[CX404|companion]] that are in possession of a [[Cold fusion|dangerous device]].

===[[The Beginning]]===
Eventually, multiple soldiers are seen in the Brotherhood of Steel usurped [[Filly]], where [[Maximus]] delivers the [[Critic|fake head]] to Elder Cleric Quintus.

Some soldiers are later seen preparing for the upcoming [[New California Republic|NCR]] [[Griffith Observatory|base]] invasion until their [[Brotherhood officer]] sends them off.

Upon arriving at the NCR base, multiple soldiers are seen fighting alongside their Brotherhood knights and officers against the [[NCR soldier (TV series)|NCR soldiers]].

When they reach the base, they're confronted by [[the Ghoul]], who kills off one of the knights, and another knight creates a blackout by shooting at a power generator. In the covert of darkness, the Ghoul kills off several knights and perhaps soldiers and officers too.

In the aftermath of the battle, several corpses of the Brotherhood soldiers are seen laid across the battleground. 

Afterward, when [[Dane (TV series)|Dane]] accompanied by several soldiers reach the room where Maximus is standing beside [[Lee Moldaver]]'s body. Dane concludes that Maximus killed her and would thereafter be anointed as a knight, thereby leading a chant in Maximus' honor with their fellow soldiers, much to Maxmimus' chagrin.

'''Tommy''' is a lead farmer {{In|FOTV}}.

==''Fallout'' TV series==
===[[The Radio]]===
Tommy and his father [[Adam (TV series)|Adam]] use metal detectors to collect small metal objects in the desert. When they return home, Tommy empties their finding into a pot to be melted down.

When they enter their home, they find [[the Ghoul]] eating a meal at their table. Things escalate when the Ghoul mentions his older brother [[Roofus]]' name and pulls out an envelope. Adam demands to know what Tommy did and what was in that envelope.

The Ghoul then unfolds a blood-splattered letter with a hole in the center and claims that it was Tommy who got Roofus involved with the "madwoman," [[Lee Moldaver]]. He'd go on reading off a blood-splattered letter that said Roofus entrusted Tommy to deliver a stash of caps to a courier to deliver an [[Siggi Wilzig|Enclave defector]] to Moldaver. When the Ghoul found Roofus' body and picked up the envelope with the letter inside, he couldn't read it because of the blood-splattered hole in it. The Ghoul then asks Tommy to inform him of Moldaver's location, and he'll leave.

Tommy apologizes to his father as Adam demands that Tommy tell him what the Ghoul wants to know, but instead pleads that he doesn't want to spend all of his life digging through dirt. On the other hand, Adam forces Tommy to tell the Ghoul what he wants, or they will all be killed, including [[Sandra (TV series)|his sister]].

Under pressure, Tommy reveals that Moldaver was at the [[Griffith Observatory|observatory]]. With that, the Ghoul asks Tommy if he would really allow him to leave knowing what he did to his older brother, which earns him a cold stare by Tommy. Adam claims he will not, but the Ghoul is unconvinced that Tommy would not try in the future. Just then, Tommy tries to reach for the [[flintlock musket]] beside him, but the Ghoul uses his [[Dom Pedro (TV series weapon)|revolver]] to shoot him dead.

As the Ghoul leaves, Adam mourns the loss of his son.

'''The Ghoul''', born '''Cooper Howard''', is a central character {{In|FOTV}}, along with [[Lucy MacLean]] and [[Maximus]]. Once a famous [[Hollywood]] actor in the pre-[[Great War|War]] era, after the bombs fell, he was transformed into a [[ghoul]], spending centuries fighting and killing in the [[wasteland]] as a [[bounty hunter]]. By [[Timeline (2250-2300)#2296|2296]], he is one of the most notorious killers around the greater [[Los Angeles]] area, respected and feared for his deadly marksmanship and unscrupulous beliefs.

The Ghoul also appears in the ''[[Burning Springs (update)|Burning Springs]]'' update for ''[[Fallout 76]]'', where he offers bounty hunting quests to the [[Vault Dweller (Fallout 76)|Vault 76 dwellers]].<ref name="burning springs reveal">[https://archive.ph/hwiF9 Bethesda: "Get a first look at Burning Springs, coming soon to Fallout 76!" (archived)]</ref>

Cooper eventually proposed to Barb during a vacation to [[Catalina]], after going to what he described as a "romantic point" along the rocks which he remembers as being "covered in seagull shit." Nonetheless, it was a success, as Barb accepted his proposal and eventually married him.<ref>[[The Ghoul|Cooper Howard]]: ''"Last night I was thinking about when I proposed to you. Catalina. ===Military service to acting career===
[[File:CooperHowardT45.png.png|thumb|right|250px|Cooper in his T-45 power armor, at the Alaskan front.]]
In 2066, not long after Janey's birth, the [[Sino-American War]] began. Cooper served in the [[United States Marine Corps]] and was deployed to the [[Alaska]]n frontline to fight the [[China|Chinese]] incursion, witnessing first-hand how close the American territory came to falling into enemy hands until the deployment of [[West Tek]]'s [[T-45 power armor]], which he himself would be equipped with. While he believes the power armor was instrumental to the US' subsequent defense, Cooper also experienced the armor's numerous design flaws, which included a vulnerability in the chestplate welding that caused many of his comrades to lose their lives.<ref name="CooperBudT45">[[Bud Askins]]: ''"Mr. Howard, great work today."''<br/>Cooper Howard: ''"Ah. Oh, thanks. Thanks, man."''<br/>Bud Askins: ''"Bud Askins. I oversee our Southern California operations."''<br/>Cooper Howard: ''"Ah."''<br/>Bud Askins: ''"I, uh, came over to Vault-Tec in Q3 after a ten-year stint at West Tek."''<br/>Cooper Howard: ''"West Tek."''<br/>Bud Askins: ''"It's a defense contractor."''<br/>Cooper Howard: ''"Oh, I'm, uh, very familiar with you guys. You designed the T-45 power armor."''<br/>Bud Askins: ''"First of its kind. No, I-I oversaw the-the rollout. You know, the design flaws were ridiculous, but they sure looked great."''<br/>Cooper Howard: ''"I wore the T-45 when we almost lost the great state of Alaska to the Reds. Those design flaws of yours cost a lot of good men and women their lives."''<br/>Bud Askins: ''"Yeah. Product management was never my bag."''<br/>({{FOTV|1|6}})</ref>

[[File:FOTV S2E4 PLA soldiers.png|thumb|319x319px|Cooper being confronted by [[People's Liberation Army]] soldiers on the Alaskan front]]
Cooper himself was nearly killed by these armor flaws. During a recovery mission on an Alaskan island, he was ambushed by Chinese forces while his T-45 suit was suffering severe damage and malfunctions that quickly rendered him defenseless. Cooper was saved only by an unnatural weapon of war: a [[Deathclaw (TV series)|deathclaw]], though he had no way of knowing what it was at the time. The creature ripped his aggressors to pieces and seemingly came close to doing the same to him until it was drawn away by nearby fighting. This would become a lasting source of trauma for Cooper, though he would have no way of understanding what the creature was until many years later.<ref>{{FOTV|2|4}}</ref> This incident aside, on the other hand, the armor also saved his life at times; at one point when Cooper's squad was attacked and he was wounded, his T-45 protected him long enough for his comrade [[Charles Whiteknife]] to drag him away to safety.<ref>[[Charles Whiteknife]]: ''"Now, remember, these are the 45s. They're hard as hell to walk in. And this Power Armor's so goddamned hot from the fires that it's melting the snow beneath my feet. And I'm dragging this guy, you know, just dragging him through the mud and the slush. And I don't know if this poor damn grunt is dead or alive. So, the first chance I get, I take cover, take a knee, open up that visor. And who do I see staring right back at me? It's Cooper Howard. That's right. You know what he said to me? Coop. You remember?"''<br />Cooper Howard: ''"Uh, 'How do I look?' "''<br />Charles Whiteknife: ''"That's a lie, ladies and gentlemen. That is a lie. I mean, the movie star Cooper Howard, he probably would have said that. But the man I was looking at... he looked up at me and he said, 'How are the others?' Nearly burnt alive... and he was worried about our squad. Fuckin' Marines, huh?"''<br />({{FOTV|2|3}})</ref>

Following his discharge from the military and return to California, Cooper entered an acting career at [[Hollywood]] in [[Los Angeles]].<ref name="FOTV0101" /> Over the subsequent decade, he established himself as a high-profile actor with a penchant for [[Motion pictures|Western movies]], such as the ''[[A Man and His Dog]]'' series (where he co-starred with the canine actor [[Roosevelt]], whom he eventually adopted as his pet)<ref name="FOTV0106">{{FOTV|1|6}}</ref> and ''[[The Man from Deadhorse]]''.<ref name="FOTV0103" /> The latter movie was rewritten partway through filming to depict more jingoistic American values and make Cooper's character display no remorse against a defeated enemy; though Cooper was uncomfortable with this change, he eventually relented and shot the scene as directed, and the movie went on to perform spectacularly.<ref group="Non-game">[[Fallout - A Special LIVE Report from Galaxy News]], ~46:00: ''"The Man from Deadhorse gallops to a fast start at the box office! The Howard-led Western is said to be the next smash for California Crest Studios."''</ref>

[[File:FOTV Cooper thumbs up.png|thumb|250px|left|Cooper's signature pose]]

Shortly after his initial conversation with Dale, Cooper made a far more fateful media appearance; at the insistence of Barb, who had, by that time, become a high-ranking executive of the [[Vault-Tec Corporation]], he agreed to participate in a Vault-Tec promotional campaign having him don the signature [[Vault jumpsuit]]. After striking a few poses, Howard settled on a pose smiling with his thumb raised up, a gesture that would become heavily associated with his public image (for good and bad) over the years.<ref name="FOTV0103">{{FOTV|1|3}}</ref>

===Involvement with Vault-Tec===
Once the promotional campaign kicked off, Cooper's involvement in Vault-Tec marketing increased steadily, as he began making appearances in television advertisements promoting the [[Vault]]s. While the company's pay was high, Cooper found himself losing out on parts in his old movie genre, as other actors found his work as the so-called "pitchman for the end of the world" distasteful. His first experience with a boycott was when his fellow castmates refused to leave their trailers with him on set, which actress [[Bonnie Lewis]] attributed to "moral grounds." In private, Cooper's role as a corporate mascot started to wear him down, and he felt particularly disdainful towards the socially disconnected Vault-Tec executives he met. One of these encounters occurred during the shoot of a commercial for [[Vault 4]], when Cooper met [[Bud Askins]] and listened to him boasting about his role in overseeing the rollout of the T-45 power armor at West Tek, which particularly drew Cooper's ire due to his own experience with the armor.<ref name="CooperBudT45" />

[[File:The Trap Cooper at wrap party.png|thumb|250px|Cooper brushes off Askins at a wrap party]]
Barb subsequently organized a wrap party at their home; though Cooper sent invitations to many other Hollywood celebrities, everyone snubbed him over his ties to Vault-Tec except for fellow actor [[Sebastian Leslie]], who had also chosen to take up advertising work. Leslie further derided their detractors by claiming that "radicalism" was sweeping Hollywood, with even Charles Whiteknife (who had also taken up acting after his military service) holding alleged communist ties.

Despite his friend's reassurances, Cooper's doubts remained; Barb also tried to assuage his skepticism, citing how well their work for the company was providing for them, and that their daughter could even join the company's ranks one day. However, against Barb's hopes, Cooper still grew disillusioned, second-guessing his commitment to city life and Vault-Tec.<ref name="FOTV0106"/>

===Fracturing family ties===
These difficulties caused a rift to form between Cooper and Barb, as she shut down any discussion of leaving the company at her husband's request. During one exchange, she let slip that there were ingrained [[Societal Preservation Program|differences in the Vaults]] and that her high-level position at Vault-Tec guaranteed her family a spot in one of the "good" ones, something their wealth could not otherwise guarantee.

Later, Cooper secretly reached out to Whiteknife to confide in his concerns over the corporate influence on his life. While still considering himself an anti-communist due to his experiences in the Sino-American War, the exchange with Charles was enough to make Cooper agree to go with his friend to attend a meeting of the social "radicals."<ref name="FOTV0106"/>

[[File:FOTV S1 Ep6 Barb Thomas.png|thumb|250px|left|Cooper annoyed that Vault-Tec will not allow dogs into the Vaults, in an apparent revision of their rules.]]
Cooper's continued pushback against Barb's work strained their married life, as he started openly criticizing Vault-Tec in front of her, in particular his view of their rules as being increasingly draconian, such as a ban on dogs. Barb refused to understand his reservations, pointing out that she was working to save her family from the possible nuclear annihilation and revealing that she was working to get them into a "special" Vault reserved for management.<ref name="FOTV0106"/>

Distraught at his family's fracturing and mounting skepticism over Vault-Tec, Cooper chose to attend the meeting of Charles' group at the [[Hollywood Forever]] mausoleum, where he was introduced to the group's leader, a woman named [[Lee Moldaver|Kate Williams]]. Williams' rhetoric initially repulsed Cooper, especially when she equated the average American with the average [[China|Chinese]] citizen to point out class tensions within the U.S. Cooper was frustrated at being dragged to what he saw as a meeting of secret communists, but when he tried to make a dramatic exit, Williams convinced him to stay by suggesting that his wife was keeping secrets. Speaking with Williams in private after the meeting, Cooper learned of his wife's involvement with Vault-Tec in buying out Williams' [[cold fusion]] research, something which could promise to put an end to the [[Resource Wars]]. While he refused to be directly recruited into recovering Williams' cold fusion, he still held onto the secret listening device she gave him as a "souvenir."<ref name="FOTV0107"/>

===Learning the truth about Vault-Tec===
The doubts fueled by Williams about Barb's possible duplicity continued to gnaw at Cooper, eventually driving him to test out the listening device on his wife's [[Pip-Boy]] at home. Though the device worked, Cooper was repulsed by the thought of eavesdropping on his wife and threw away the device; however, haunted by misgivings over his role in advertising for Vault-Tec, he retrieved the device from the trash later that night, making up his mind to spy on Vault-Tec.<ref name="FOTV0107">{{FOTV|1|7}}</ref>

[[File:FOTV s1 ep8 Young Betty.png|thumb|250px|Cooper helped to his room by a young [[Betty Pearson]]]]

On the day that Cooper put this plan into action, he drove Barb to the [[Vault-Tec headquarters (TV series)|Vault-Tec headquarters]] in Los Angeles where she worked, and soon chose to enter the building after realizing he could not a clear signal to Barb's Pip-Boy from outside, where he was guided by a young [[Betty Pearson]] to a guest room, ostensibly to wait for his wife. 

Cooper's plan was perfectly timed for him to listen in on a secret conference hosted by Barb and Bud Askins for representatives from several other prominent U.S. corporations. In the meeting, Barb pitched the idea of letting these corporate partners use the Vaults to run social experiments on their inhabitants to create the perfect post-apocalyptic society, in the spirit of capitalist competition, and it culminated with Barb proposing that Vault-Tec could incite the apocalypse themselves to ensure a return on investment for the corporations. Left in shock over the revelation of Vault-Tec's true intentions, Howard barely registered his subsequent meeting with Barb's personal assistant [[Hank MacLean|Henry "Hank" MacLean]], one of Bud Askins' trainees and an example of Vault-Tec's perfect society.<ref name="FOTV0108">{{FOTV|1|8}}</ref>

===Plotting against Robert House===
Left reeling from the news, Cooper left the building before Barb could see him. His initial reaction was to skip town with Janey and Roosevelt, planning to drive away to hide in Bakersfield under the guise of a family vacation. However, they ended up stopped in the middle of a suburban neighborhood right as a nuclear alert warning system test began, causing a panic and delaying their travel.

Cooper chose to meet with Kate Williams, revealing what he had learned at the Vault-Tec meeting. Williams took the opportunity to try to bring Cooper into her agenda, telling him not to flee, and instead to stay with Barb and accompany her on her upcoming trip to [[New Vegas|Las Vegas]], [[Nevada]], where she supposedly would deliver the cold fusion technology to [[RobCo Industries]] CEO [[Robert House]]; Williams' intent was for Cooper to retrieve the cold fusion technology and assassinate House, though Cooper was not willing to commit murder. Williams let him go with the thought that, if he did not act, he would be allowing House to start the apocalypse. Giving up on fleeing, Cooper returned home, keeping up an act that all was well in front of Barb.<ref>{{FOTV|2|1}}</ref>

Sometime between then and the trip to Las Vegas, Cooper attended a fundraiser hosted by the [[American Veteran Entertainment Alliance]], in which Charlie Whiteknife was to be honored; the two discussed Williams' plans, with Charlie trying to persuade Cooper to go through with assassinating House. During the ceremony, Cooper met two figures: one was [[Diane Welch|Congresswoman Diane Welch]], the well-meaning but ineffectual representative for the district of [[Glendale]], California; the other was Robert House, whom Cooper did not recognize due to the man's use of a [[Robert House's double|body double]] for public appearances. House, remaining incognito, discreetly implied he was aware of Charlie and Cooper's "pinko" leanings and subtle threatened Cooper against interfering with his plans, but Cooper taunted him and left. Soon after, Charlie told a speech about one of his shared wartime experiences with Cooper to imply to Cooper that he should go with the plan against House, even giving Cooper the ceremonial lighter he had just been awarded as a symbol of acting for the greater good.<ref>{{FOTV|2|3}}</ref>

As such, Cooper ultimately stuck with Williams' plan to travel to Las Vegas. However, when he privately met with Williams again at [[Los Angeles International Airport]] just ahead of the plane to Las Vegas, he still decided not to fully commit to the plan, telling Williams he would recover her cold fusion technology without killing House. This change came after Cooper saw that Hank, Barb's assistant, was handcuffed to a suitcase, causing him to suspect that Hank was the carrier for the cold fusion tech.

In Las Vegas, Cooper and Barb were driven to the [[Lucky 38]] to attend the defense contractors' summit being hosted there. Outside, Cooper noticed Representative Welch being manhandled by [[Military Police officer]]s for protesting against corporate influence over politics; he helped her up and encouraged her to continue her opposition before entering.

Inside the casino, Cooper tried to get close to Hank but was diverted by Robert House's body double, who revealed himself as such to Cooper and explained that the actual Mr. House wanted to see him. Cooper went to the [[Lucky 38 penthouse]], where he met the [[Securitron]] [[Victor (Securitron)|Victor]] and was re-introduced to the genuine Robert House, recognizing him from their impromptu meeting before; House explained that he believed his and Cooper's destinies were "mathematically intertwined." Though Cooper was reluctant to speak with his target face to face, House enticed Cooper by revealing that he had also seen the "demon in the snow" that Cooper had encountered in Alaska via spyware planted in the military's T-45 power armor; he posited that it was connected to the imminent nuclear war that he believed Barb was also involved in.

House further explained to Cooper that he sought the cold fusion technology for his plan to survive the nuclear apocalypse, and that he did not intend to work with Vault-Tec to '''start''' war, as Cooper believed. He also elaborated on why he saw himself as "mathematically intertwined" with Cooper: House's algorithms had concluded nuclear war would be inevitable on April 14, 2065 -- the same day that Cooper and Barb's daughter Janey was born. Cooper's arrival in Las Vegas with Barb was also unforeseen by House; it moved up the predicted date of the coming war by a full month, convincing him that Cooper played an essential role in causing the war. Finally, House also told Cooper that he believed there was "another player at the table," not Vault-Tec or any of their corporate partners, that would actually start the war.

====Stealing cold fusion====
Cooper was skeptical of all of House's claims, and the meeting ended with him deriding House as a lunatic and House, in turn, angrily accusing Cooper of being a "killer" who could end up being responsible for ending the world. Left greatly upset by the meeting, Cooper got heavily drunk, causing a spectacle on the ground floor of the Lucky 38 until Barb brought him back to their hotel room to sober up. When he finally did, Cooper decided it was time to confront Barb over her involvement in Vault-Tec's conspiracy.<ref>{{FOTV|2|5}}</ref>

Their talk initially went poorly as Barb tried to defend her actions while Cooper could not look past the willingness of the woman he loved to kill millions, including families just like them. Eventually, though, they came to a more honest conversation where Barb revealed how truly terrified she was and that she was not doing all of it by choice, but instead because a [[Siggi Wilzig|representative]] of a [[Enclave|powerful group]] had placed a threat against her family if she did not comply with their demands to steer the Vault-Tec meeting as they wished. Even as he understood his wife's struggle more clearly, Cooper still could not shake off the betrayal he felt and chose to take action to steal the cold fusion and stop Vault-Tec's deal with House. His plan came out to getting Hank hammered, then trying to steal the diode; however, this ended up requiring Barb's help, revealing to Cooper that his wife was still willing to act against Vault-Tec for their family's sake.<ref>{{FOTV|2|6}}</ref>

With the cold fusion diode in his hands, Cooper sought out Diane Welch as the only "good" person in the American political strata he knew of, but was not reassured by her proposal to give away infinite energy to the public. He did accept her alternative plan to give it to the [[Last President of the United States|U.S. president]], thinking it would stop House and Vault-Tec's plans, while hiding the details of his wife's cooperation. That night, with Barb's full awareness, he met with Welch and the president in a discreet limousine and handed over the diode, an act for which the president commended him.<ref>{{FOTV|2|7}}</ref>

By the next day, while the couple were in [[Camp McCarran|McCarran Airport]] preparing to leave Las Vegas, Cooper received a call from House, warning him of coming consequences but reassuring him that House was not the one who was responsible. Cooper was then arrested by [[U.S. federal agent]]s under the authority of the [[House Un-American Activities Committee]] as a suspected communist. Realizing that the president -- in reality a member of the [[Enclave]] -- had set him up, against Barb's wishes, Cooper took the fall alone to protect her, ordering his wife to deny any knowledge of his actions for their daughter's sake.<ref>{{FOTV|2|8}}</ref>

===Entertainer work===
The accusations put against Cooper by the HUAC made him a social pariah. While it is unclear what exactly happened next, it is known that Vault-Tec completely replaced Cooper as their mascot with a cartoon figure named [[Vault Boy]]. Cooper and Barb also were publicly believed to have divorced. Separated from his former social circles and financial status, Cooper was forced to take work as an entertainer at kids' parties. Although they shared custody of Janey, Cooper was reportedly forced to pay alimony to his ex-wife, and he was left reliant on his gig work to make money.<ref>[[Frank (TV series)|Frank]]: ''"Why the hell is Cooper Howard working kids' birthday parties?"''<br />[[Bob Spencer]]: ''"What else? Alimony."''<br />{{FOTV|1|1}}</ref> Furthermore, since discovering the truth about Vault-Tec and his government, Cooper refused to do his iconic thumbs-up pose, outwardly citing the increasing global tension towards nuclear war with China. Janey helped her father with his entertainer work whenever she stayed with him, learning the ropes of the trade and forming an even deeper bond. 

[[File:FOTV Trailer 1 59.png|thumb|250px|left|Saving Janey on the day of the Great War]]
On October 23, 2077, the day of the [[Great War]], Cooper was performing at eight-year-old [[Roy Spencer]]'s birthday party in the [[Hollywood Hills]] near [[Los Angeles]] with his daughter when the city was struck by four nuclear warheads. Cooper tried to flee to safety with Janey by riding out together on the back of his horse [[Sugarfoot]].<ref name="FOTV0101" />

===After the Great War===
In the ensuing events, Cooper somehow ended up being separated from Janey, with the fates of his daughter and ex-wife unknown to him; Cooper himself ended up transforming into a [[ghoul]], though it is unclear if it was from radiation exposure or another method. He came to believe that his family had managed to get to safety inside either a regular Vault or a "[[List of known Vaults#Management Vaults|special Vault for management]]," but he did not know where,<ref name="management">[[Lucy MacLean]]: ''"I'd ask why my dad ran into a den of giant monsters, but you'd probably just tell me to shut the fudge up."''<br />[[The Ghoul]]: ''"[sighs] Before the war, there was a plan... for a special Vault. A Vault for management. That's where my wife is. Where my daughter is, too."''<br />Lucy MacLean: ''"You never found it?"''<br />The Ghoul: ''"Oh no, I found one, in California. It was empty. Found another one in Oregon. It was empty, too. Turns out, there's more than a few special Vaults for management sprinkled across the Wasteland. All of 'em empty. But I've never found one in Vegas."''<br />Lucy MacLean: ''"So, you think if my dad's in there, maybe your wife and daughter are, too?"''<br />The Ghoul: ''"Uh-huh. And now, they're surrounded by these fuckin' things."''<br />({{FOTV|2|5}})</ref> and that finding someone related to Vault-Tec could potentially lead him to them, but this never fully materialized for over two centuries.

Life in the post-War world greatly changed Cooper. Cooper was forced to become a real outlaw in the vein of his old Westerns, a crack shot with a firearm and a deadly threat to his enemies. Over the years, "Cooper Howard" disappeared into the personality of "the Ghoul," a hardened, pragmatic, and amoral mercenary willing to do whatever it took to survive, even cannibalism. As the Ghoul, he became notorious for his work as a [[bounty hunter]] to keep himself afloat and his skills sharp while continuing to search for his family. His ghoulish condition eventually began to slide into [[Feral ghoul|feralhood]], which led to him becoming dependent on the use of [[inhaler vial]]s to stave off the mental degeneration.

===Return to Los Angeles===
====The hunt for Siggi Wilzig====
Around 2296, the Ghoul was rescued from Dom Pedro's stronghold by a trio of bounty hunters led by [[Honcho]], who wanted to recruit him to help them claim the bounty on deserted Enclave scientist [[Siggi Wilzig]], who possessed a critical artifact and was heading for Los Angeles -- the Ghoul's old home. The Ghoul chose to attack Honcho and his team rather than work with them, but decided to take up the hunt for Wilzig on his own anyways.

By mid-2296, the Ghoul had returned to the Los Angeles area. Determining that Wilzig would pass through the settlement of [[Filly]], the Ghoul chose to enter the town and lie in wait for his target, even though the town did not allow ghouls inside.<ref>[[Ma June]] (to the Ghoul): ''"You know your kind ain't welcome around here."''<br />({{FOTV|1|2}})</ref> Soon, Wilzig indeed arrived to make contact with [[Ma June]], a shopkeeper who had been hired to direct Wilzig to his ultimate destination; [[Vault 33]] dweller [[Lucy MacLean]] was also present, seeking details about the woman who had kidnapped her father from their Vault.

The Ghoul chose to publicly confront Wilzig and Ma June right outside [[Ma June's Sundries|her store]] in the middle of town. Failing to pacify them, the Ghoul shot off Wilzig's foot, crippling him; to stall the Ghoul, Ma June announced that she would pay anyone who could kill the Ghoul, leading to a firefight between the Ghoul and multiple [[Filly townsperson|townspeople]] who tried to claim the new price on his head, with the Ghoul quickly coming out on top. Lucy then shot the Ghoul with a tranquilizer dart, hoping to stop him nonlethally, but this was ineffective. Despite this, the Ghoul was still prevented from reaching Wilzig when [[Maximus]] (masquerading as [[Titus|Knight Titus]]), a member of the [[Knights of San Fernando]], intervened.

Though the Ghoul was eventually able to incapacitate Maximus by damaging his [[T-60 power armor (TV series)|T-60 power armor]], the distraction allowed Lucy to escape with Wilzig. The Ghoul was still able to track them after he healed [[CX404]], Wilzig's dog who the Ghoul had shot earlier in the fight. By the time they reached Wilzig, Wilzig had already died, with his headless corpse left behind at a crashed [[Soviet satellite]] near Los Angeles International Airport. The Ghoul decided to continue the search for Wilzig's head, with CX404 taking the lead.

The Ghoul eventually caught Lucy MacLean near the flooded remains of [[Hollywood Boulevard]], where she was trying to recover Wilzig's head from a [[Gulper (TV series)|gulper]] that had swallowed it. The Ghoul tried to use Lucy as bait for the gulper, but this ended with his supply of inhaler vials being lost after Lucy used his satchel to defend herself from the creature. This forced the Ghoul to change priorities, and he left to acquire a new supply of vials, with the captive Lucy in tow.

On the way, the Ghoul treated Lucy harshly as a means of exposing her to the realities of wasteland life, even cutting off her finger when she bit off one of his, though he was briefly intrigued when Lucy revealed her last name was "MacLean," the same as that of his wife's former assistant. At their destination, a [[Super Duper Mart (TV series)|Super Duper Mart]] in [[Santa Monica]] used by a pair of organ harvesters, the Ghoul traded them Lucy in exchange for vials, sending her inside and then collapsing due to his deteriorating condition. Events in the store led to the organ harvesters being killed and Lucy escaping alive; though the Ghoul was defenseless, Lucy chose to show him mercy, leaving him a vial. Once recovered, the Ghoul entered the Super Duper Mart and took the organ harvesters' remaining vials for himself.

====Finding a lead====
The Ghoul was still at the Super Duper Mart when [[Sheriff (TV series)|sheriffs]] from the "[[Govermint]]" arrived, holding him responsible for killing the organ harvesters and taking him into custody. At the "Govermint's" headquarters, the Ghoul discovered his old associate Sorrel Booker was leading the gang. Booker wanted the Ghoul dead, but the Ghoul turned the tables on Booker's henchmen, though he left Booker alive. He then discovered a wanted poster for [[Lee Moldaver]], whom he recognized as Kate Williams from before the Great War.

Determined to track down Moldaver, the Ghoul hunted down [[Roofus|a messenger]] working for her faction. Unable to get the answers he wanted, the Ghoul visited Adam, the messenger's father, where he finally learned that Moldaver's hideout was at [[Griffith Observatory]] after threatening Adam's family and killing another of his children, who wanted revenge for his brother's death.

The Ghoul arrived inside Griffith Observatory not long after Lucy, who had come to deliver Wilzig's head, and the artifact inside, to Moldaver in exchange for her father, the captured Vault 33 overseer Hank MacLean -- the same Hank who the Ghoul had known before the Great War. At the same time, the Knights of San Fernando were also converging on the observatory, intent on claiming Wilzig's artifact for themselves. The Ghoul wiped out most of the [[Brotherhood of Steel]] chapter's advance unit, hindering their progress, though Maximus was able to escape and reach Moldaver's control room. There, Maximus freed Hank but was then knocked out by him after he put on a deceased knight's T-60 power armor.

The Ghoul entered the room just as Hank was reaching out to Lucy, who was emotionally conflicted after having just discovered her father had orchestrated the destruction of the city of [[Shady Sands]]. The Ghoul shot and wounded Hank, having recognized that this was the same Hank he had known from before the war, before asking Hank about his family's whereabouts. Instead of replying, Hank fled, though the Ghoul allowed him to do so. Recognizing that Lucy would prove useful as Hank's daughter, the Ghoul allowed her to join him and Dogmeat (his name for CX404) to hunt down Hank, who had fled to the Mojave Wasteland.

===Tracking Hank MacLean===
The Ghoul's reason for letting Hank escape was because he believed that Hank would lead him to wherever his family was located -- if not directly, then at least to somebody who might know. This theory seemed to proven correct when he found that Hank's destination was [[New Vegas]], the city which Robert House had made his life's goal to protect; Cooper viewed House as dangerous, and suspected that Hank was trying to reconnect with him, assuming (rightfully) that Vault-Tec could have hidden one of its secret "[[Las Vegas management Vault|management Vaults]]" underneath the city and that Hank or House could lead him to it.

Through the journey from Los Angeles to New Vegas, the Ghoul gradually opened up to Lucy. Even though they still mutually disagreed with each other -- the Ghoul viewing Lucy as soft and too idealistic, and Lucy viewing the Ghoul as callous and overly brutal -- over their approaches, they still influenced each other. The Ghoul went so far as to share some details about his past and goals and, in some circumstances, willingly place his life in Lucy's hands.

This showed in a scam that they pulled multiple times to obtain supplies for their journey: taking advantage of the fact that he had made numerous enemies over the years, Lucy would bring the Ghoul to one of his enemies, pretending she had captured him, and then collect whatever bounty they had on the Ghoul; the two of them would then drop the act and kill all of the enemies before escaping with their loot. The [[Great Khans]] controlling [[Novac]] were one such group of victims; by that point, Lucy had grown sick of the Ghoul's aggressiveness and attempted diplomacy even though it ruined their plan (and threatened the Ghoul's life), but she still ended up helping the Ghoul slaughter the raiders when all else failed.

Along the way, the pair ended up taking a significant detour when, against the Ghoul's wishes, Lucy opted to explore an [[Affordable Al's Discount Hospital|abandoned hospital]] in response to a cry for help. The Ghoul acquiesced, but when he discovered that the people seeking aid were slaves from [[Caesar's Legion]], he decided they were not worth helping; however, he failed to explain himself to Lucy, which caused her to leave him behind after they were attacked by [[Radscorpion (TV series)|radscorpions]] and Lucy chose to use the last of her supplies to heal the wounded slave instead of the Ghoul. She intended to come back for him after sending the slave to safety, but she was instead captured by the Legion.

The Ghoul managed to recover and track down Lucy, though he realized he could not take on the Legion alone and chose to seek out the NCR for help, justifying this uncharacteristic behavior as due to needing Lucy for later. He was surprised to find [[Camp Golf]] abandoned, though a chance encounter with Victor pointed him to rumored NCR remnants elsewhere. However, these hopes were dashed when he found the remnants and discovered that an isolated trio was all that was left of the NCR, not nearly enough to take on the full Legion camp. Though he was sympathetic to the NCR's cause, he refused to help and instead decided to expose them to the Legion in exchange for Lucy's freedom. However, spurred by his pre-War memories of Charles Whiteknife's words, the Ghoul again acted uncharacteristically by setting off explosives in the Legion camp to protect the NCR remnants; this caused the fractured Legion to descend into [[Legion civil war|active infighting]], delaying them enough for the Ghoul to return to and warn the NCR remnants.

Once Lucy had recovered, they left for New Vegas and soon reached the outskirts of the Strip. Though the Ghoul was apprehensive, seeing the exterior abandoned save for ghoulified [[Kings]], Lucy's [[Buffout (TV series)|Buffout]] addiction-fueled bravado spurred them to enter the Strip. They soon discovered the Strip was infested by deathclaws, forcing them to flee into [[Freeside]] before the Ghoul could investigate the Lucky 38. Realizing that the deathclaws made it impossible to proceed, and with the condition of the Strip seemingly suggesting that Robert House was dead (something Victor had told him at Camp Golf), the Ghoul retreated into drinking at the [[Atomic Wrangler Casino]] while sending Lucy off to cure her addiction. It was at this point that Hank sent [[Snake oil salesman|one of his mind-controlled victims]] to negotiate with the Ghoul; Hank seemed to confirm the Ghoul's suspicions, claiming that Barb and Janey were cryonically preserved in a management Vault underneath New Vegas, and that he would harm them if the Ghoul did not bring Lucy back to Vault 33.

The Ghoul ultimately chose to betray Lucy, shooting her with a tranquilizer to prepare for the journey back, though the act caused him to feel immense guilt for the first ever since becoming the Ghoul.<ref group="Meta">[[Walton Goggins]]: ''"He has to do what he has to do in order to find out what he needs to find out about his own family. But they both care for each other. It is the first time, I think, The Ghoul has ever felt... guilty."''<br />("[[Go Inside Episode 5 - Season Two]]")</ref> However, Lucy stayed awake long enough to retaliate by punching the Ghoul out of a window, causing him to be impaled on a street pole and nearly killing him before she was taken into the management Vault by Hank.

The Ghoul only barely survived thanks to the intervention of a [[hooded super mutant]], who hauled him off the pole and away to an [[adobe church]], healing the Ghoul's wound with a chunk of [[uranium]]. The super mutant sought to recruit the Ghoul to a cause preparing for war against the Enclave and/or humanity; the Ghoul refused, citing his independent streak and mission to find his family. As such, the mutant chose to knock out the Ghoul and leave him at the [[Mojave Mission School]], where he was later found by Maximus and [[Thaddeus]], who were brought there by CX404.

Knowing that Maximus and Lucy were in love, the Ghoul joined forces with Maximus and Thaddeus to get past the deathclaws on the Strip for their intertwined goals: Maximus sought to rescue Lucy from the Las Vegas management Vault, while the Ghoul sought to get into the Vault to find his family. Maximus and Thaddeus had just deserted from the Brotherhood after having stolen the cold fusion diode, a lucky coincidence which perfectly fit the Ghoul's plans. The Ghoul led the group to an [[Run-down town|abandoned NCR armory]], where they stocked up on weaponry for the upcoming battle, and the Ghoul also managed to acquire the cold fusion diode from Maximus in exchange for presenting him with a suit of [[Ranger power armor|specialized NCR power armor]].

With Maximus acting as a human shield against the deathclaws in his power armor, the Ghoul was able to enter the Lucky 38, where he used the cold fusion diode to activate Robert House's [[Artificial personality|AI backup]], negotiating with House to get into the Las Vegas management Vault. House, "accompanying" the Ghoul along the way via a Pip-Boy, explained that the Enclave was still manipulating events from the shadows and accused the Ghoul of having enabled them to rise to power when he inadvertently gave them cold fusion before the Great War.

Inside the management Vault, the Ghoul found and saved Lucy from her father, who had been just about to implant her with a [[brain-computer interface chip|mind control chip]]. Though he wounded Hank, he left it up to Lucy to deal with Hank as she saw fit.

Soon, the Ghoul reached the cryo chamber bay where his family was supposedly preserved; however, the pods intended for his wife and daughter were empty. However, Barb's pod contained a postcard of [[Colorado]], seemingly as a message to Cooper about her location. Though House tried to belittle Cooper, Cooper instead renewed his hope after having finally found a clue that his family might have survived. He rejected House and left with CX404 towards Colorado in search of his family.

The bounty hunter realized that Roger was on the verge of going feral, and so he showed him a bit of kindness, reminding him of memories of pre-War foods like ice cream and apple pie and his mother's cooking, then shot him through the head, much to Lucy's horror. After stripping the body for valuables including teeth, the Ghoul started removing pieces of flesh to make "[[ass jerky]]," meanwhile asking Lucy questions about herself and becoming curious about her last name "MacLean." When Lucy upset the Ghoul by remarking on his crude behavior carving up Roger's body, he responded by forcing her to do it instead.

Once they were closer to the Super Duper Mart, Lucy's growing thirst compelled her to drink from a highly irradiated puddle, inviting further mockery from her captor over the Vault dweller becoming more like a wastelander like himself. Outside the Super Duper Mart, the Ghoul placed his order for a six-month supply of inhaler vials, then ordered Lucy inside at gunpoint to allow the harvesters to examine her "merchandise" before he could get his vials. As soon as Lucy entered the building, he collapsed, exhausted and weakened from withdrawal.

After Lucy re-emerged from the Super Duper Mart, having freed the organ harvesters' captive ghouls, including feral ones that slaughtered them, she discovered the catatonic bounty hunter on the ground. After briefly considering shooting him with her [[10mm pistol (TV series)|pistol]], Lucy remarked that, even if she ended up a ghoul herself, she would never become a monster like him, leaving a supply of inhaler vials behind with the Ghoul as a show of her sticking to the Golden Rule.

The Ghoul was hauled from the Super Duper Mart to the [[BBQ Shack (TV series)|BBQ Shack]] serving as the headquarters of the "[[Govermint]]," a low-level racketeering operation run by [[Sorrel Booker]], whom the Ghoul knew in the past. During their sitdown, as Booker began spouting about the damage to his reputation that killing the harvester ring would incur, the Ghoul ignored him in favor of using thread to sew Lucy’s severed finger onto his own hand. After taunting [[Troy (TV series)|Sheriff Troy]] about his father's death in Filly, the Ghoul disarmed both him and [[Rex (TV series)|Sheriff Rex]], killing both while sparing his old associate. On his way out to resume hunting, the Ghoul happened upon a wanted poster for [[Lee Moldaver]] and realized that she highly resembled the "Miss Williams" he knew from the pre-War era, inciting him to try and get answers about her.<ref name="FOTV0106"/>

====[[The Radio]]====
Seeking answers about Moldaver, the Ghoul visited a lead farmer named [[Adam (TV series)|Adam]] at his home in the desert plains. While threatening the lives of his son [[Tommy (TV series)|Tommy]] and daughter [[Sandra (TV series)|Sandra]], the bounty hunter revealed a letter he had recovered from Adam's older son [[Roofus]] after killing him in Filly that proved a link to the Madwoman. Once Tommy was forced by his father to give up Moldaver's stronghold at the [[Griffith Observatory]], the Ghoul goaded the young man into trying to go for his [[flintlock musket]], giving him an excuse to shoot and kill him before leaving.

Later on, while making his way to Los Angeles and the Observatory, the Ghoul happened upon the [[Red Rocket (TV series)|Red Rocket station]] where [[Thaddeus]] had abandoned CX404 by locking her in a [[Nuka-Cola]] cooler unit. After rescuing her, the canine now named "Dogmeat" joined him in loyalty, though in a quiet moment at the ruins of Hollywood Forever, he expressed that he still missed and would continue to miss his old pup [[Roosevelt]].<ref name="FOTV0107"/>

====[[The Beginning]]====
Arriving at the Griffith Observatory sometime around the start of the [[Battle of Griffith Observatory|battle]] between the [[Brotherhood of Steel (Lost Hills)|Brotherhood of Steel]] and the [[New California Republic]] remnants under Moldaver's command, the Ghoul hid inside the main building. After the Brotherhood pushed into the observatory, slaughtering many [[NCR soldier (TV series)|NCR soldiers]], the bounty hunter revealed himself. Upon explaining his knowledge of their power armor's weak spot due to his old military experience, the bounty hunter fired a penetrating round into one of the [[Knight (TV series)|knights]] through their T-60 suit, killing them instantly, before escaping into the shadows after extracting the building's generator's [[Fusion core (TV series)|fusion core]]. This then allowed him to ambush and wipe out most of the Brotherhood vanguard, including multiple knights and [[Shortsight|Petty Officer Shortsight]], temporarily pushing back the assault's progress.<ref name="FOTV0108"/>

Shortly after Lucy reunited with Maximus and was holding her father [[Hank MacLean|Hank]] at gunpoint upon finding out he had destroyed the NCR capital of [[Shady Sands]] and turned her [[Rose MacLean|mother]] into a ghoul, Hank was shot from behind by the Ghoul. Taunting the Vault overseer with an iconic line from his old films, he briefly reminisced about meeting Hank over 200 years ago when he was a Vault-Tec executive, then demanded to know the whereabouts of his family; Hank however refused to answer, instead fleeing from the observatory in a stolen T-60 suit. The Ghoul then told a somber Lucy that, by letting her father run away, he might leave a trail leading straight to the real mastermind behind the Great War and the suffering of millions that it caused. The bounty hunter then extended the offer to "come and meet [her] makers," which Lucy eventually agreed to after mercy-killing her ghoulified mother.

The duo later escaped the Brotherhood's advance on the observatory via a secret path into Hollywood Forever, being joined by Dogmeat as they began the journey chasing after the escaped Hank towards the [[Mojave Wasteland]] and [[New Vegas]].<ref name="FOTV0108"/>

====[[The Innovator]]====
Sometime after entering the [[Mojave Wasteland]], the Ghoul and Lucy ran low on supplies but were able to reach [[Novac]], which they discovered had been taken over by a group of [[Great Khans]] led by [[Nick the Prick]]. Correctly believing that the raiders still held their generations-long grudge against him, the Ghoul devised a plan with Lucy to have her turn him in for the reward, then take the first opportunity to free him from his captivity so they could kill the raiders and take the rest of their supplies. Lucy evidently agreed to this, as the Ghoul is first shown already bound with a noose around his neck, standing on makeshift gallows in the swimming pool of the [[Dino Dee-lite Motel]].

The Ghoul mocks the Great Khans' hatred and tries to signal for Lucy to shoot off the rope from her vantage point in [[Dinky the T-Rex]], but she misses the cue even after he repeats it. Realizing she is deliberately stalling, he curses right before the gallows drop. The Ghoul manages to shout through his asphyxiation but is ignored as Lucy attempts to negotiate peacefully with the Khans. Once she realizes that will not work, Lucy finally uses her [[Semi-automatic rifle (TV series)|rifle]] to sever the noose. After dropping into the pool, the Ghoul goes for cover while deriding Lucy's reluctance to kill. The pair start a shootout, with Lucy sniping from the statue and noticeably only inflicting non-fatal wounds on the raiders. The Ghoul, however, goes straight for the lethal option, using various means and weapons like his [[Knuckle Duster]] to take out the raiders one by one, while Lucy shakes her head in frustration at the bounty hunter's brutal tactics. Once they collect the slain raiders' supplies, the Ghoul uses a cracked [[Fusion core (TV series)|fusion core]] to heal the wound on his neck inflicted by the noose as they depart with [[CX404|Dogmeat]].

The three walk across a flat desert plain, where the Ghoul and Lucy bicker over Lucy's reluctance to kill her enemies, which ruined the plan to rob the Great Khans. Eventually, they reach a hill overlooking New Vegas, with Lucy commenting that the city looks virtually unchanged from its pre-War days. The Ghoul reveals that the city's air defenses shot down most of the missiles targeting it during the Great War. However, when Lucy questions why "they" did not do that for the rest of America, the Ghoul sneers that there was no "they," and Vegas only survived because of one man - Robert House. Lucy assumes that the Ghoul's family headed to Las Vegas because it is safe, but the Ghoul counters that nowhere around House is safe. As they continue walking, the Ghoul evidently begins thinking back to his old history with House.

Later, Lucy and the Ghoul continue arguing until they come across the body of a wastelander who was crushed to death by Hank in his stolen [[T-60 power armor (TV series)|T-60 power armor]]. Further along, they encounter a roadside soup shack run by an elderly woman, [[Old Woman Gretch|Gretch]]. Despite the Ghoul's initial plan to threaten her, Lucy gets him to stand by while she tries a diplomatic approach and learns that Hank took Gretch's [[Sunburned man|son]]. The Ghoul remarks that Gretch's son is most likely dead, which she agrees with.

Following Gretch's directions, they eventually find Hank's footprints taking them to the site of an abandoned [[Starlight Drive-in Theatre (TV series)|Starlight Drive-In Theatre]], where the Ghoul is momentarily distracted seeing that his old film ''[[A Man and His Dog|A Man and His Dog 3]]'' was listed on the marquee. He then notices Lucy entering [[Vault 24]], exposed behind the wrecked screen display. Inside the abandoned foyer of the Vault, the Ghoul admits that he's been to many others before, every time dreading that he might finally find evidence to confirm whether Barb and Janey are alive, dead, or "something far worse." The Ghoul then forcefully drags Lucy to make her use her Pip-Boy to reactivate the elevator; he notices that Hank's footprints show him both entering and leaving the Vault, leading the Ghoul to realize that Hank came here looking for something. He also finds a discarded wool cap with a red star that he shows to Lucy before they take the elevator deeper into the Vault.

On a lower level, the pair discover the skeletal remains of the Vault's residents and the true nature of its [[Vault#True purpose|experiment]]: brainwashing American citizens into [[Communism|communist]] sleeper agents via [[Brain-computer interface chip|black box chips]] and propaganda materials. The Ghoul looks around the grisly aftermath as Lucy tries to look for more clues on the Vault terminals but finds that Hank extracted the holotape data and locked down the system. In an operating theater, they find a box of [[Sugar Bombs]] deliberately left behind on a gurney. A sudden noise alerts them to the presence of Gretch's missing son, outfitted with a black box chip by Hank. Using the man as a living loudspeaker, Hank urges Lucy to return to Vault 33, referring to his nickname for her, "Sugarbomb." Once the message ends, the man's chip overloads, causing his head to explode and splattering Lucy and the Ghoul in his blood. Seething with rage, Lucy decides to defy Hank's warning, telling the Ghoul she is determined to put a stop to her father's schemes.

====[[The Golden Rule]]====
After leaving Vault 24, the trio continue towards New Vegas. Lucy remarks on the bounty hunter's rudeness, such as snatching away her water canteen, arguing that if they do find his wife and daughter, they probably would not like the kind of person he turned into. This irritates the Ghoul at first but noticeably hits a nerve as he tries to move on. Their journey is then interrupted when they hear a [[Wounded woman|woman's]] distant calls for help from the nearby ruins of [[Affordable Al's Discount Hospital]]. The Ghoul wants to ignore what he thinks is an obvious trap and chides Lucy over her "stupid" refusal to ignore a cry for help; when she goes in anyway, he begrudgingly follows after her and [[CX404|Dogmeat]].

Inside the hospital ruins, Dogmeat leads the pair towards the room where they find the wounded woman. The Ghoul points out her tunic marked with a red X, remarking that she is "awful far west," but does not elaborate further. Lucy offers the use of her last [[Stimpak (TV series)|stimpak]] to the woman against the Ghoul's advice, with him claiming people like them don't deserve it. He then notices a [[wounded man]] nearby groaning in pain. As he offers a hand to the man, he draws his [[Bowie knife (TV series)|Bowie knife]] and slits the man's throat, to Lucy's shock, then cuts off a [[Cannibalism|piece of flesh to eat]]. However, the Ghoul pukes it up shortly after. He returns to check the man's corpse, revealing a poisoned wound on his back. The two then hear something scuttling around in the building's ventilation and loud bangs in an adjacent room. Soon after, a few [[Radscorpion (TV series)|baby radscorpions]] emerge and attack. While they are able to fend off the smaller creatures, the banging sounds in the nearby room get louder as something rams against the doors. The Ghoul is thrown to the floor as a bigger, mature radscorpion bursts in. He initially avoids getting stung, using his knife to sever one of its mandibles. He scrambles back and pulls the woman over him as a shield, causing her to get stung. Lucy pulls her back to save her, causing the Ghoul to get stung in the leg. Before the creature can attack him again, the Ghoul grabs a [[Frag grenade (TV series)|frag grenade]] from his satchel, stuffing it into the wounded mandible, and kicks the radscorpion away right before it blows up.

Lucy checks on the two of them, both suffering from the effects of the radscorpion's venom and begging her to use the stimpak. Lucy decides to inject the woman, both because she knows the Ghoul's regeneration will keep him from dying and because she sees it as duly deserved for his cruelty. She then leaves him behind while taking the woman out of the ruined building, though the Ghoul warns her that her "golden rule" will come back to bite her. She instead promises to come back for him, solely to prove him wrong about being kind to others.

====[[The Profligate]]====
After being left behind inside the ruined hospital, the Ghoul fought through the painful effects of the radscorpion sting to crawl into a pool venue. With Dogmeat beside him for company, he takes out a [[skinning knife]] to extract the stinger tip that was left inside his thigh from the creature's attack. He keeps himself from blacking out from the pain by talking to the dog, using a warped "ship of Theseus" metaphor to describe himself, until he is able to dig out the stinger and throw it away. As he recovers, the Ghoul remarks that Dogmeat is the first eing in a long time that he feels is worth talking to about his deeper emotions.

The Ghoul is later able to track Lucy down to the [[Caesar's Legion camp|Mojave camp]] used by [[Caesar's Legion]]. From a ridge close by, the Ghoul observes through a spyglass as Lucy languishes in her [[crucifixion]], then notes with surprise that the Legion has two Caesars and realizes he is going to need help for his plan. He temporarily leaves the Legion camp behind to travel to the [[House Resort]], formerly occupied by the NCR as [[Camp Golf]]. Outside, while dosing on an [[inhaler vial]], he says to Dogmeat that he is not looking to help Lucy for her sake, but only because she will be useful for later. He walks through the exterior tents but finds them completely deserted; the resort itself is also abandoned.

In one of the resort's side rooms, a loud noise reveals the presence of [[Victor (Securitron)|Victor]], a [[Securitron (TV series)|Securitron]] whom the Ghoul recognizes, as he calls him by name. He finds that Victor has been left with a stuttering speech and difficulty remembering his own name, which Victor attributes to time and damage to his memory banks, also saying he does not recall why he is in the House Resort. The Ghoul asks him where to find the NCR to help with the Legion, but Victor says they have been absent for a while, remarking that both factions continue to fight each other, and the Legion against themselves, over control of the Mojave. The Ghoul changes topics to Robert House, whom Victor claims is "gone," but the Ghoul says they both know better than to think that House would not have planned for everything. Victor indicates that some of House's plans, made before the Great War, were impacted when he was unable to acquire the cold fusion technology, further implying that the Ghoul himself was involved in this. As the Ghoul turns to leave, Victor mentions that a group of [[New California Republic Rangers|NCR Rangers]] have been hiding out in "the hills," giving the Ghoul his next clue.

Following Victor's clue, the Ghoul crosses the Mojave and eventually reaches a site in the mountains near the town of [[Primm]], walking to a spot littered with rusted car husks as Dogmeat begins growling at something nearby. It is then revealed that they are being watched by a female sniper wearing old [[NCR armor]] who fires a shot over the bounty hunter's shoulder and identifies herself as [[Rodriguez|Captain Rodriguez]] of the NCR. The Ghoul turns to someone else approaching him before a man wearing NCR Ranger combat armor bashes him with his rifle butt and threatens to shoot him, with a second Ranger observing far behind. Rodriguez, however, intervenes, having recognized the Ghoul and further claims that he and the NCR "go way back;" despite the violent treatment, the Ghoul laughs triumphantly at having found the NCR.

The Ghoul's hopes are dashed when he arrives at the [[NCR outpost|NCR holdouts' camp]] and realizes that the three of them are the only ones there. He remarks on the dilapidated circumstances, with Rodriguez responding that by the present, their three-man team is the only active NCR force around that they are aware of. She remarks that time has taken a toll on some more than others, gesturing to one of the Rangers, [[Biff (TV series)|Biff]], as he prods at a caged [[Squirrel (TV series)|squirrel]]. She reveals that around the time of the [[fall of Shady Sands]], they knew about the presence of an [[New California Republic Army|NCR Army]] battalion to the east, but the Ghoul counters that they may no longer be there given how long ago Shady Sands was destroyed.

Rodriguez tries to plead for the Ghoul's help to find their lost reinforcements and help fight the Legion, but the bounty hunter mocks their hopes of winning with their low manpower despite Rodriguez' assurances that they have the advantage. Seeing him begin to cough, Rodriguez offers the Ghoul a tin of inhaler vials and tries to convince him to fight for a bigger cause than himself, one she implies he used to share. The Ghoul takes the vials but seemingly rebuffs her plea; he leaves with a remark that, if the NCR's future has really died in the Mojave, it deserved to.

The Ghoul returns to the Legion camp, passing by the weakened Lucy, who spots him through her blurry vision. Inside one [[Caesar (TV series)|Caesar's]] tent, the [[Lacerta Legate]] questions the Ghoul about the information he claimed to offer to them. The Ghoul initially tries to negotiate as the legate threatens him, and the deal works out when the bounty hunter reveals he can tell them where Rodriguez' NCR holdouts are hiding. The Ghoul reflects on memories of his old friend [[Charles Whiteknife]] as he observes Lucy being freed at his request, part of the deal he made with the Legion, then carries the weakened Vault dweller out of their camp. On the way past a war rally led by Caesar declaring the imminent eradication of the NCR, the Ghoul notices a stash of [[Dynamite (TV series)|dynamite]] nearby and seemingly forms a plan.

As the Ghoul and Lucy walk away, they stop on the ridge overlooking the Legion camp. The Ghoul takes out the commemorative lighter Charlie gave him long ago, serving as a reminder of what he could and should do to protect the ones he loved. The Ghoul laments his choices before both watch as an explosion rocks the camp, revealing that he rigged the dynamite stash to explode. The blast is mistaken for an attack as both Legion factions erupt into fighting, consequently delaying their attack on the NCR outpost, as Lucy and the Ghoul depart.

====[[The Demon in the Snow]]====
To help Lucy recover from her ordeal, the Ghoul brought her to the NCR holdouts' camp, where they supplied her with a treatment of [[Buffout]]. He sat close by when the Vault dweller awakened two days later in the midst of the Rangers packing up their camp, likely to leave before the Legion can hunt them down. He chimes in when Rodriguez asks Lucy about the nature of her search, whether she's looking for someone she loves or someone she hates, as Lucy gives a nervous, vague answer to avoid having to reveal they're looking for [[Hank MacLean|her father]], who was responsible for destroying Shady Sands. On their way out, Lucy says to the Ghoul that she thought the Rangers were nice, although he reminds her that they likely would not be if they knew she was related to, and hunting down, the man responsible for destroying their home.

Sometime after leaving the NCR outpost, Lucy has started undergoing withdrawal symptoms such as scratching, erratic thoughts, heavy breathing, and general irritability. She asks if the Ghoul knows that feeling, which he affirms, revealing that she has become [[Buffout addiction|addicted]] to the Buffout used to rejuvenate her. He claims that she has two options ahead of her; the first is to completely avoid more drugs and fight through the withdrawal symptoms, which will take days. The other option is simply doing more drugs, the Ghoul's favorite, as the drug-addled Lucy talks herself into it and takes the bounty hunter's offer of more Buffout, then resumes the walk towards New Vegas.

On the outskirts of the [[New Vegas Strip]], a jittery Lucy looks around while the Ghoul observes the gate onto the Strip ahead but remarks that it is not what he was expecting; Lucy jokingly asks if he was expecting a "welcoming committee," but he confirms that he was. Through his spyglass, the Ghoul spots a wrecked [[Securitron]] unit, then looks at a large group of [[Feral ghoul (TV series)|feral ghouls]] wearing the outfits of their former gang, the [[Kings]], loitering around the entrance. Seeing this, he suggests they turn and head for the other entrance on the east end, going through [[Freeside]], but Lucy protests that the half day's walk will take too long. She goes on ahead to the nearby entrance, to the Ghoul's chagrin, though he admits his begrudging respect for Lucy's newfound, drug-fueled bravado.

As they approach, a few of the ghoulified Kings notice and growl at Lucy while she tries to be diplomatic, but when one of the ferals snarls loudly at her, she instinctually raises her [[Lever-action rifle (TV series)|lever-action rifle]] and shoots off its leg. Though initially surprised by her reflexive violence, the Buffout-addled Lucy gleefully starts a fight with the remaining ghouls while the Ghoul and Dogmeat watch nearby. He nearly draws his knife to defend himself from a pair of ghouls climbing over the car he was next to, but Lucy shoots them dead first, surprising him. After a few more kills, she gloats to the Ghoul, though he is unnerved when she comments it's okay to kill the ferals because they're "just" ghouls. Once the last of the ghouls is killed, the bounty hunter brushes off Lucy's subsequent request to try cigarettes as he looks around at the destroyed robots wondering what happened, while the Vault dweller tries to steady her jittery hand as they get ready to enter New Vegas.

As night falls, Lucy and the Ghoul walk through the deserted New Vegas Strip; Lucy comments on how she expected to see more people around, and the Ghoul agrees. He reiterates that they should go around to Freeside, but Lucy opts to loudly shout out for Hank. The Ghoul walks ahead, following Dogmeat as he looks up at the lit rooms of the Lucky 38. He then picks up on the unusual presence of radiation on the Strip thanks to Lucy's Geiger counter going off. When Lucy shows a large, cracked eggshell she found to him, all of the blood drains from the Ghoul's face as he realizes the imminent danger they're in, right as they both hear loud roaring coming from nearby.

Lucy's Buffout-charged bravado has her believe they can take on whatever the threat is, but the Ghoul is stricken with fear, looking towards the glass door entrance of [[Gomorrah]] and a monstrous shadowy figure moving inside. Lucy notices the uncharacteristic terror on the Ghoul's face as her cheer falls away and she becomes more aware of the danger. Once the massive deathclaw rips the casino's doors off and advances out onto the streets, it notices the pair and roars as it stands up to its full height, causing Lucy to mutter a nervous "okey-dokey."

====[[The Wrangler]]====
As they face the deathclaw emerging from Gomorrah, the pair notice a second one approaching from the left, followed by the roars of a third coming from the right. Lucy defers to the Ghoul's advice, and he simply tells her to run. They flee behind a wrecked taxi car while seeing a fourth one munching on some meat nearby. Lucy asks the Ghoul to give her more drugs to use, but he refuses, sarcastically claiming that they would need a rocket launcher to stand a chance against the creatures. As a deathclaw begins to crawl over the car to find them, the Ghoul notices Dogmeat running through a gap in the nearby gate and gets an idea. He packs some [[Frag grenade (TV series)|frag grenades]] into a duffle bag, throws it in the opposite direction, then shoots it to cause an explosion. Once the deathclaws are distracted, the Ghoul leads a full sprint to the gate, managing to slide under and pull Lucy out after him as they escape into Freeside.

By daytime the next day, the two observe the gate into the Strip and the deathclaws roaming beyond it, to which Lucy asks if they had always been there and the Ghoul responds in the negative. Lucy wonders on why her father would go into a den of monsters. The Ghoul reveals that for years, he searched for the "special Vault for management" mentioned by Barb, hoping that his wife and daughter were preserved there, only to discover that there were many of them all across the wasteland, but all of the ones he has found have turned up empty. Now, the hunt for Hank has convinced the Ghoul there is one in Las Vegas, beneath the Strip, but he is stopped just shy of his goal by the deathclaws.

They stroll down the streets of Freeside, where Lucy admits that she is starting to lapse into withdrawal again. Fed up with Lucy's drugged antics, the Ghoul hands her a load of caps to take to a store called [[Sonny's Sundries]] where she can buy some [[Addictol (TV series)|Addictol]] to cure her condition; in the meantime, he is going to get a drink and get "fucked up." The Ghoul enters the [[Atomic Wrangler Casino]] for a bottle of [[Alcohol|whiskey]]. The barkeep [[Shotgun Jeff]] tries to strike up conversation, remarking on the deathclaws infesting the Strip as nothing more than the latest "change in management," but the Ghoul firmly interrupts to tell him to stop talking. The bounty hunter downs one drink after another as he slides back into his memories of his former life and family. He later takes the remainder of the bottle with him as he goes to find a private room.

When Lucy returns from her short shopping trip, on the verge of another vomiting episode due to the effects of her Addictol dose, he points her in the direction of the open window and holds her steady as she upchucks out onto the street, then helps her to a seat. As Lucy gives her thanks, however, he points out the presence of the [[snake oil salesman]] in the room, cleaned up and dressed up as a representative sent by her father and controlled via a [[Brain-computer interface chip|black box chip]]. The salesman claims that the facility Hank is in indeed contains the preserved Barb and Janey, and that her father is threatening to sabotage their cryonic suspension and kill them if the Ghoul does not return Lucy to [[Vault 33]] for her own safety while Hank completes his plans. The bounty hunter, while regretful of having to betray Lucy, chooses to save his family; he shoots Lucy with her own [[Tranq gun (TV series)|tranq gun]] to ease the journey back to her Vault.

However, as the Ghoul is furnishing another drink, he is caught by surprise when Lucy fights through the tranquilizer's effect and punches him through the window with a [[Power fist (TV series)|power fist]] she stole from Sonny's. He falls towards the street and gets impaled through the abdomen on a metal pole, left dangling there as Lucy is then found in the hotel room by Hank and captured.

====[[The Other Player]]====
Unable to escape from his predicament alone, and entirely ignored by Freeside's citizens, the Ghoul struggles to reach for his satchel containing his vials but finds them just out of reach. He tries to get Dogmeat to get them for him, but when the canine instead picks up his hat, his anger causes him to lapse into a fit of [[Feral ghoul|feral]] snarling, resulting in Dogmeat running away as he shouts at her for being a "useless dog." He flails around for a bit longer on the pole until the strain causes him to pass out.

By nighttime, the Ghoul still has made no progress getting free while starting to suffer more frequent bouts of feral behavior. He tries to fight against it, first reminding himself of his name, then telling himself about his daughter, repeating her name and that she is alive. This manages to focus his mind long enough to regain his senses as he attempts to pull himself up the length of the pole he is impaled on. Fighting through the pain, the Ghoul nearly manages to reach the top before he abruptly lapses into his feral state and loses his grip, falling back to the bottom and being rendered unconscious again.

Sometime later, as the Ghoul continues to hang limp, a hulking figure wearing a concealing hooded cloak approaches him. They grasp the top half of the pole and easily tear it off, then pull him from the remainder and carry him away. The Ghoul briefly awakens to find himself being dragged on a sled through a forested area, with his rescuer approaching an [[adobe church]] decorated with [[Meat bag (Fallout 4)|meat bags]] filled with bloody remains. He fully regains consciousness a short time later as the figure pulls the sled he's tied to up into a vertical hanging position, then removes a glowing chunk of [[Uranium ore (Fallout 76)|uranium]] from a lantern. In a deep voice, the figure tells him that there is a war coming and they need him healthy, right before jamming the uranium inside his open wound and telling him to stay still so the uranium can heal him.

The figure sits nearby, telling the Ghoul that while humans may consider them "abominations," humans were the ones that created both of their kind. The Ghoul remarks on the difference between them, but the figure laughs, saying that ghouls and mutants are "kin" and should be united against "[their] common enemy," the ones who drove them to near extinction and then forgot about them. The Ghoul tries to reach for his [[Mare's Leg (TV series)|rifle]] nearby, but a boulder thrown against a wall warns him against it. The figure moves to stand in front of him while saying that the war coming will be against the ones who set events in motion and brought about the wasteland: the [[Enclave]].

The Ghoul says that he has only ever worked in the wasteland alone, but the figure remarks on how poorly that has turned out for him. A bit of Cooper Howard comes out as the Ghoul says that his only reason to continue day after day has been to find his family. The figure accepts that, saying the Ghoul will need friends, while leaning into the light of a torch to reveal himself as a [[Hooded super mutant|super mutant]]. He tells the Ghoul that, if he will not join the mutants' side against the Enclave, then he won't be allowed to know where they live, before using a bludgeon to knock the Ghoul out and transport him away to a safe location.

Sometime later, the Ghoul is found in a shed at the [[Mojave Mission School]] by [[Maximus]] and [[Thaddeus]] after being led there by Dogmeat, who also returns his hat to him; the Ghoul makes his displeasure to see the Brotherhood soldier again known immediately.

====[[The Handoff]]====
Sometime after he is discovered by Maximus and Thaddeus, the two ex-Brotherhood members have begun following the Ghoul, who is leading them into a [[run-down town]] marked with NCR flags. Thaddeus tries to ingratiate himself to the Ghoul as a fellow ghoul, but the Ghoul ignores him. Maximus asks where the Ghoul is leading them, and he tells them that he is taking them to New Vegas so that they can save Lucy from the management Vault under Las Vegas. Thaddeus points out that they are walking in the wrong direction, but the Ghoul explains that he is taking them to resupply and stock up on weaponry in order to get past the deathclaws on the Strip.

Maximus, seeming to just understand, asks to confirm that Lucy is in New Vegas. In exasperation, the Ghoul calls him similar to Lucy for asking so many questions. He then trains his revolver on Maximus and orders him to explain how and why he has the cold fusion diode which the Ghoul gave away in the past. Maximus only tells him that he intends to give it to Lucy, believing that she is a good person who will do the right thing with it; the Ghoul seems to accept this, displaying a slight smirk on his face, and they move on.

The trio enter a supply shack inside the town, taking multiple weapons found inside the shack. The Ghoul himself picks up several [[Frag grenade (TV series)|frag grenades]] and a [[Gauss rifle (TV series)|Gauss rifle]]. Thaddeus, again, tries to talk to the Ghoul about them both being ghouls, showing him a mouth-like mutation below his shoulder; however, this only shocks the Ghoul, and he does not answer when Thaddeus asks if it is normal.

Maximus asks the Ghoul why he is helping them. The Ghoul responds that he is offering them a "transaction," which will require them giving him the cold fusion diode in exchange for receiving the weapons needed to get to Lucy. Maximus refuses to budge from his intent to give it to Lucy, but the Ghoul explains that they will have to give the diode to a "really bad person" (actually referring to Robert House) to save Lucy, claiming that they will have to "rig the game" like House did in order to get to Lucy. As he says this, he cuts open a tarp to reveal a suit of [[Ranger power armor|NCR power armor]] underneath. Maximus, left emotional at the realization that he is symbolically returning to his origins, gives the Ghoul the cold fusion diode.

After Maximus dons the power armor, he, the Ghoul, Thaddeus, and Dogmeat return to Freeside and advance towards the gate leading to the Strip. The sight of a figure wearing NCR-marked armor causes the [[Freesider]]s around them to celebrate the return of the NCR and cheer for Maximus as a hero who will liberate them from the deathclaws; the Ghoul remarks to Thaddeus on the power of "brand recognition." Once they reach the gate, the Ghoul begins giving orders to the duo to make sure they can make it past the deathclaws, telling Maximus to move fast. He then tells Thaddeus to get into position as their sniper. However, Thaddeus' right arm falls off, making him unable to use his rifle. The Ghoul is vaguely concerned, but ultimately decides to move on, and he and Maximus leave Thaddeus behind.

Once they enter the Strip, the Ghoul tells Maximus to shield him from any deathclaws they encounter. Maximus immediately has to face off with a deathclaw in front of them; after hanging back and watching for a short while, the Ghoul begins walking towards the Lucky 38. As he reaches the door, he discovers that the deathclaw is now advancing on him after Maximus' arm became stuck in a car door. Visibly terrified, the Ghoul fires off several shots from his Gauss rifle into the deathclaw, but they barely stagger it. Just before it can attack him, Maximus decapitates the deathclaw; still in shock, the Ghoul gives Maximus a gesture of thanks, and then enters the Lucky 38 while Maximus faces off against two more deathclaws.

[[File:Securitron down mr house screen.png|thumb|right|200px|The Ghoul in the Lucky 38 penthouse]]
Inside the Lucky 38, the Ghoul reaches the penthouse and discovers it to be in disarray, with Mr. House's console only displaying a "NO SIGNAL" status. Based on what he saw House do pre-War, the Ghoul is able to open the hidden chamber with House's cold fusion reactor and activate it using the cold fusion diode. This allows Mr. House's digitized consciousness to be activated on the screen. The House AI greets the Ghoul as an "old chum," and the Ghoul's expression hardens in response.

====[[The Strip]]====
In flashbacks, Cooper and Barb partake in drinks at a lounge in [[Camp McCarran|McCarran International Airport]], pleased at having apparently stopped Vault-Tec's conspiracy to end the world, and discussing alternative plans to survive (Barb now agrees with Cooper's suggestion of Bakersfield, but Cooper now thinks of Colorado as a better option). Soon after, they learn that Hank and [[Steph Harper]] have gotten engaged. Cooper then receives a call from Robert House, who tells him that House is not responsible for what's about to happen to Cooper and that he had previously told Cooper about there being other, worse people in the world compared to House. Realizing that the U.S. president had set him up, Cooper orders Barb to let him take the fall in order to protect Janey, sharing one last kiss with Barb before he is arrested by [[U.S. federal agent|federal agents]] under the authority of the [[House Un-American Activities Committee]].

In the present, House expresses pleasure at being alive again. The Ghoul threatens to shoot the cold fusion diode, which House admits will have unknown effects, though he warns that the resulting destruction would be enough to extend to even other planets. The Ghoul questions why House hadn't told him about the management Vault in Las Vegas, which House claims was because the Ghoul had not brought him the diode yet. The two men reach a deal where House will lead the Ghoul to his family in exchange for House being left the cold fusion diode. To do so, House requires the Ghoul to don a [[Pip-Boy]], smugly saying that everyone eventually ends up working for him.

With his digital avatar on the Pip-Boy, House monologues to the Ghoul about how he had rigged the management Vault in Las Vegas to protect the city from Vault-Tec. However, he admits that he "miscalculated," because he had not known that the management Vault was actually supposed to be for the Enclave, Vault-Tec's "investors." House further admits that the Enclave completely outclassed him in terms of their plans to control the future, telling the Ghoul that the Enclave were responsible for setting the deathclaws on New Vegas.

As the Ghoul enters the management Vault, House also admonishes Cooper for having indirectly contributed to the Enclave's rise to power by having given them the cold fusion diode, and warning that they must stop Hank MacLean, who is an "acolyte" of the Enclave now using the management Vault for his own plans. Inside, the Ghoul finds Hank about to implant a Black Box chip in Lucy; the Ghoul saves Lucy by killing the [[massive legionary|brainwashed legionary]] who was restraining her. He also wounds Hank and gives Lucy his Knuckle Duster, leaving Hank's fate in Lucy's hands before leaving.

Finally, the Ghoul finds the room containing Barb and Janey's cryo pods. On his orders, House opens the pods, which are both empty. Though the Ghoul is initially devastated, he finds new hope in a Colorado postcard on the floor of Barb's pod with the message "Colorado was a great idea," a seeming reference to Cooper's suggestion of their next destination just before his arrest. Though House attempts to admonish him for holding false hope, the Ghoul rejects him, telling House that for the first time in 200 years, he knows that his family is still alive. The Ghoul then abandons the Pip-Boy and refuses to continue working with House, despite House's warnings that the Enclave is still a threat both to them and to the Ghoul's family.

Leaving New Vegas, the Ghoul heads for Colorado with Dogmeat in search of Barb and Janey.

==Personality==
[[File:FOTV Official Trailer Still 120.png|thumb|250px|Cooper kissing Barb]]

Prior to becoming a ghoul, Cooper was a loving husband to Barb and father to Janey. He considered himself a staunch believer in the American Dream and its value, having served in [[Alaska]] in the first years of the [[Sino-American War]], and a staunch anti-[[communist]] as a result. He highly valued freedom and disliked Vault-Tec's rules, positing he should have the freedom to wear a green jumpsuit instead of a blue one.

However, the changing culture and domination of megacorporations put his principles to the test. Eventually, he grew disillusioned as he did Vault-Tec advertisements and suffered ostracism from his peers. When he learned that the very corporation his wife worked for conspired to use the nuclear war to implement its own designs for the world, he lost faith and tried to make a clean break and is suggested to have suffered a messy divorce and blacklisting from entertainment work for it. 

Though these events failed to break him, he was permanently, fundamentally altered by the [[Great War]]. Ghoulified by [[radiation]], Cooper used his military training and skills as a cowboy to survive as a bounty hunter. His experience in show business led him to become the Ghoul, a larger-than-life gunslinger capable of incredible brutality, even resorting to [[cannibalism]] when needed (and saving [[ass jerky]] for later). Two hundred years of survival in the brutal wasteland changed him into a cynical, bitter, and ruthlessly pragmatic man who doesn't believe in goodness, but still has a certain "code of honor" in his drive to locate his family.

Despite his disdain of humanity, he still harbors a soft spot for dogs, as seen with his dog [[Roosevelt]], as well as CX404. Cooper saved her life, despite initially mortally wounding her while she was attacking him in Filly. He once commented his film ''A Man and His Dog'' as being his favorite and was greatly upset by Vault-Tec's rule that no dogs will be allowed in the Vaults.

Despite his moral ruthlessness, he tends to remain civil when dealing with others, even to those whom he intends harm. To this point he agrees with Lucy that torture is wrong, citing a pre-War study, and the reasoning that he would be unwilling to help someone harming him, but he uses this to reveal to Lucy that he is actually using her as bait for a gulper. He also spares those he deems not a threat to him, such as Sorrel Booker, with whom he has history, instead just killing Booker's deputies who are preparing to take him out to be executed, or waiting to let Tommy try to draw his gun on him in revenge for killing his brother but letting his father and sister live. Another example of his practicality is that he intentionally grazes Hank rather than killing him so that he and Lucy can track him down later.

Despite having become a ruthless bounty hounter, he still retains part of his humanity. After two hundred years, he still remembers some memories of his life before the Great War, and carries with him a lighter given to him by his friend and fellow veteran Charles Whiteknife.

==Behind the scenes==
===''Fallout'' TV series===
;Performance and casting
* The Ghoul's makeup and prosthetics are largely made using practical effects,<ref name="WastelandSurvival" group="Meta">[https://www.ign.com/articles/fallout-tv-series-amazon-prime-video-cast-showrunners-interview-wasteland-factions-vault-ghoul Fallout: A Wasteland Survival Guide to the New TV Series]</ref> while post-production visual effects technology was used to remove his nose in order to match the general appearance of a [[ghoul]] in the ''Fallout'' series.<ref group="Meta">[https://twitter.com/falloutonprime/status/1790105338701869361 @FalloutOnPrime on Twitter]: ''"Walton’s nose was removed with VFX, but the rest was Fallout’s incredible makeup and prosthetics team.<br />Get a closer look at their work here!"''</ref> Makeup designer [[Jake Garber]] stated that his team was specifically instructed to make the Ghoul's appearance more appealing (outside of the signature missing nose) compared to previous depictions of ghouls in the series (the so-called "walking corpse" look) so that Goggins' performance would be more noticeable instead of being hampered by looking "off-putting."<ref group="Meta">[https://www.goldderby.com/feature/jake-garber-fallout-makeup-designer-video-interview-1205908320/ Jake Garber reported by Gold Derby]</ref>
* [[Walton Goggins]] stated he avoided taking cues from narrative elements and characters seen in previous installments of the ''Fallout'' series when crafting the character and story of the Ghoul. Instead, he drew inspiration from classic Western films, such as the works of [[wikipedia:John Wayne|John Wayne]] and [[wikipedia:Clint Eastwood|Clint Eastwood]].<ref group="Meta">[https://web.archive.org/web/20231203220621/https://collider.com/fallout-walton-goggins-the-ghoul-inspiration/ 'Fallout's Walton Goggins Reveals Which Classic Westerns Inspired The Ghoul]</ref>
* The Ghoul's main stunt double in Season 1 is [[Justice Hedenberg]]. Additionally, [[Loop Rawlins]] was Cooper Howard's lasso double in "[[The End (episode)|The End]]."
* In an interview, Walton Goggins stated that during production, he had invented a backstory for the character of Cooper Howard. According to Goggins, Cooper grew up in the rural American Midwest, came from good family, and initially moved to Hollywood to be a horse stuntman but incidentally ended up becoming an actor after taking over a scene from another actor.<ref group="Meta">{{Cite web|url=https://www.goldderby.com/video/walton-goggins-interview-fallout/|title=Walton Goggins interview: 'Fallout'|work=Gold Derby}}</ref> Outside of Cooper's service in the military during the [[Sino-American War]], nothing about his past that could allude to this has been revealed in [[Fallout Wiki:Fallout canon|canon]] to date, aside from a throwaway line in "[[The Trap]]" where he reminisces about being a "real cowboy."
* In localized versions of the series, the Ghoul's voice is dubbed by Gilduin Tissier (European French), Paul Sarrasin (Canadian French), Shunsuke Takeuchi (Japanese) and René García (Latin American Spanish).
;Other
* The tin the Ghoul uses to carry his supply of [[inhaler vial]]s (before they are destroyed by the [[Gulper (TV series)|gulper]] in "[[The Head]]") is a vintage M. Melachrino & Co. cigarette tin.<ref group="Meta">[https://www.therpf.com/forums/threads/fallout-tv-series-radaway-viles-tin-box-reference-question.359453/ See this RPF thread].</ref>
* The car that Cooper is shown to drive in the pre-War flashbacks of the ''Fallout'' TV series is a 1954 [[Wikipedia:Kaiser Darrin|Kaiser Darrin]].


===Judiciary system=== 
{{Quotation|Violence is merely a tool. We use it to bring order to the Wasteland, but violence against a Brother of Steel is a sign of weakness.|[[Quintus|Elder Cleric Quintus]]}} 

[[File:FOTV Official Trailer Still 059.png|thumb|Aspirant Maximus is interrogated by [[Quintus|Elder Cleric Quintus]] and [[Titus|Knight Titus]]]]
The Brotherhood of Steel is a disciplined militaristic organization that possesses a variety of laws and regulations. These laws were recorded with the creation of the [[Codex|Brotherhood Codex]], which was laid out by the organizations founder, [[Roger Maxson|High Elder Maxson]]. All members of the Brotherhood are subject to the same body of laws. Even elders have been dismissed for violating the Brotherhood's charter or laws, such as destroying valuable technological devices without cause, violating the [[Chain That Binds]] doctrine, or murdering a fellow brother. Due to the requirement for elders and other high-ranking members to set an example, they may also be stripped of their position if allowing them to retain it would compromise the morale or integrity of a given chapter.<ref name="elderdis">[[Hidden Valley bunker terminal entries#Elder Dismissal Incidents]]</ref> The Brotherhood will conduct necessary investigations into individuals or incidents of interest. Evidence of guilt is required before action can be taken or a sentence given.<ref>[[Kells|Lancer Captain Kells]]: ''"I've received reports that supplies have been disappearing from our supply depot at the airport. I suspect this may be an inside job. They filed the reports. This is now a security matter. And security is your job, Paladin. Gavil will fill you in on the details. But from my perspective... the strangest thing is what was stolen. Rations. Only rations. Follow every lead. Look for anything suspicious, no matter how minor. But understand, treason is a serious charge. If someone in the Brotherhood is responsible for this, I need proof before I can act."''<br /> ([[BoSLancerCaptainKells.txt|Lancer Captain Kells' dialogue]])</ref> The organization will even interrogate the accused to determine their motives and level of guilt.<ref name="s1e1" /> Regardless, until proven guilty or innocent, every member of the Brotherhood is entitled to fair trial before the elder of a given chapter.<ref>[[Lancer Captain Kells]]: ''"You just... killed him? He was a member of the Brotherhood, Paladin. He had a right to a trial before the Elder."''<br />([[BoSLancerCaptainKells.txt|Lancer Captain Kells' dialogue]])</ref>

[[File:Clarke-in-jail.jpg|thumb|Clarke awaits trial in Brotherhood holding cells at [[Boston Airport]]|left]]
Penalties for minor offenses, such as theft or insubordination, could result in disciplinary actions like latrine duty,<ref>[[The Courier]]: ''"Why do the records show that the gun was turned back in?"''<br />[[Stanton]]: ''"Still, I haven't been exiled yet, or worse, assigned to latrine-cleaning duty. But I will, if you don't help me find that gun."''<br />([[HVInitiateStanton.txt|Initiate Stanton's dialogue]])</ref> demotion or imprisonment for more significant incidents.<ref>Lancer Captain Kells: ''"I'll send some Knights to arrest him and secure the evidence. He has a right to a trial before the Elder."''<br />([[BoSLancerCaptainKells.txt|Lancer Captain Kells' dialogue]])</ref> For more serious offenses against the organization, such as harming another member, punishment can range from exile or banishment to execution.<ref name="s1e8">{{FOTV|1|8}}</ref> Justice is often dispatched by one's superior officer. In some rarer cases, disciplinary action may be dispensed by a lower ranking member should a higher ranking member violate the Chain That Binds doctrine,<ref name="elderdis" /> the [[Codex]] or the organization's oath taken by each member.<ref>[[Felix (TV series)|Cleric Felix]]: "Aspirant Maximus. It is your most sacred duty to protect the Brotherhood. After which, it is your most sacred duty to protect the mission. After which, it is your most sacred duty to protect your lord Knight Titus. Do you accept?"<br />({{FOTV|1|1}})</ref><ref>[[Daniel Shin|Knight Daniel Shin]]: ''"It hasn't been a matter of 'if' for some time now. Only a matter of 'when.' I'm fully prepared to uphold the integrity of the Brotherhood, in whatever way Rahmani makes necessary."''<br />([[Daniel Shin/Dialogue|Knight Shin's dialogue]])</ref> 

Actions that betray the organization or harm fellow members are viewed with the highest level of severity. Those guilty of treason or those who pose a certain security risk can face execution for their actions against the Brotherhood. Again even in this case, a member of the organization accused of treason has the right to a fair trial.<ref>[[Kells|Lancer Captain Kells]]: "You just... killed him? He was a member of the Brotherhood, Paladin. He had a right to a trial before the Elder."<br /> ([[BoSLancerCaptainKells.txt|Lancer Captain Kells' dialogue]])</ref> If a situation is deemed critical, it may require the involvement of the [[Circle of Steel]], a section within the Brotherhood dedicated to the organizations internal affairs. It will task its agents with hunting down and eliminating the guilty party. This was seen when ex-Elder [[Elijah]] of the Mojave chapter was marked for death by the Circle of Steel following his failure and desertion at [[Helios One]].

[[File:FO76 steelreign chronicsonictonic 08.jpg|thumb|A captured [[Edgar Blackburn|Dr. Blackburn]] is interrogated by the Brotherhood]]
Other acts that are seen as an executable offenses by Brotherhood are crimes against humanity and experimentation on human beings.<ref>[[Daniel Shin|Knight Daniel Shin]]: ''"Those who commit crimes against humanity must be punished. It wouldn't be right to let them run free, not to the people they have killed, and not to the people they could kill in the future. The Brotherhood of Steel must protect the future of Appalachia, and of the world."''<br />([[Daniel Shin/Dialogue|Knight Shin's dialogue]])</ref><ref>The [[Lone Wanderer]]: ''"So... Any thoughts on the Enclave?"''<br />[[Gunny|Paladin Gunny]]: ''"Killing unarmed civilians... Makes me sick. Come over here, try fighting someone who shoots back, fucking cowards."''<br />([[PaladinGunny.txt|Paladin Gunny's dialogue]])</ref> This utter disdain of FEV and similar experimentation can be traced back to the foundation of the organization. In October, 2077, men under command of Brotherhood of Steel founder, [[Roger Maxson]] mutinied and executed scientist at the [[Mariposa military base]]. This was perpetrated after it was discovered that the United States government had been experimenting on prisoners of War with the [[Forced Evolutionary Virus]].<ref>[[Captain Maxson's diary]]</ref> In 2104, members of the Appalachian chapter of the Brotherhood were forced to put a stop to [[Dr. Blackburn|Dr. Blackburn's]] inhumane FEV experiments. The organization's doctrine displays that members are willing to destroy vital technology or data should it be deemed dangerous to the survival of the human race.<ref>[[Daniel Shin|Knight Daniel Shin]]: ''"How many dozens have you killed? Do you really think your life is worth more than theirs because of your education? We don't need your knowledge if you're deranged enough to resort to human experimentation."''<br /> ([[Daniel Shin/Dialogue|Knight Shin's dialogue]])</ref>

Additionally, an action that is deemed forbidden by the organization, even to those within it, was the post-War use of large-scale [[nuclear weapons]]. Even as last resort, this was seen as an unacceptable by the Brotherhood's founder, Roger Maxson, who stated, "The death, the destruction, the end of the World. That came from the nukes. There will always be a reason to use a weapon. Always. But nukes? Never again!" He would even lament that he wished he could erase the technology from mankind's future knowledge. Thus to the Brotherhood, using these weapons was seen as extremely hypocritical to their tenants and regressive to humanity's salvation.<ref>[[The nuclear option (holotape)|The nuclear option]] holotape</ref>

===Religious elements===
Generally, the Brotherhood has existed as a secular organization, although they have always had some religious motifs due to taking their inspiration from medieval knightly orders. They have often been derided by outsiders for worshipping technology, as many of their members revere the importance of lost technology without fully understanding how they function or what role they played in pre-War society.{{cn}} These religious elements have fostered within the Brotherhood for over a century; in 2161, a reactionary cabal could have taken over Lost Hills if not for the leadership of Rhombus, in which case they could have formed a techno-religious dictatorship known as the [[Steel Plague]].<ref>[[Fallout endings]]: ''"The Brotherhood of Steel, under new leadership after the death of Rhombus, becomes an overzealous, techno-religious dictatorship. In 20 years, the Steel Plague devastates the newly formed New California Republic, and starts a Dark Age that could last a thousand years"''</ref>

Over time, some elements within western Brotherhood chapters also deified the Maxson line, and by 2287, they had formed cults dedicated to worshiping Arthur Maxson (who had been sent to the East Coast with Lyons' expedition to avoid unspecified internal conflicts over thirty years earlier). These cults were not supported by the main leadership on the West Coast, who attempted to eradicate them wherever they appeared.<ref name="MaxsonBackstory" />

These religious elements became more entrenched in the 2290s, with a class of [[clerics]] who handled ideological, ceremonial and seemingly also spiritual affairs emerging within at least some Brotherhood chapters. These clerics became the leadership of one minor chapter, the [[Knights of San Fernando]] on the [[West Coast]], which caused the chapter to develop a particular view of the Brotherhood's mission as divinely righteous.<ref>[[Felix (TV series)|Cleric Felix]]: ''"Aspirant Maximus. It is your most sacred duty to protect the Brotherhood. After which, it is your most sacred duty to protect the mission. After which, it is your most sacred duty to protect your lord Knight Titus. Do you accept?"''<br />({{FOTV|1|1}})</ref><ref>[[McRae|Yosemite elder]]: ''"I'm not interested in fighting a rebellion, Quintus."''<br />[[Quintus|Elder Cleric Quintus]]: ''"But the Brotherhood began as an act of rebellion. Before the war, an ordinary captain was sent to a research center in California. Roger Maxson. And when he saw what they were doing there... depraved experiments on human subjects, well, then, Roger Maxson had to ask himself, who did he serve? His God? Or his government? He chose his God. He shot the scientists. He shot the people who tried to stop him. His government had defied God, and for that, he defied his government. But he wasn't punished. He was saved. So that he could found our noble order. If our rebellion is righteous... the way will be paved for us."''<br />({{FOTV|2|3}})</ref> Clerics also came into use by the Commonwealth Brotherhood, the dominant Brotherhood chapter of the time, though their clerics were still subordinate to their soldier class.<ref>[[Maximus]]: ''"What do you want?"''<br />[[Xander Harkness|Paladin Xander Harkness]]: ''"Well, my arrival seems to have put a damper on the conversation. Your Clerics won't even look at me. You guys have a lot of Clerics, you know that?"''<br />Maximus: ''"Yeah, I guess we do."''<br />Paladin Xander Harkness: ''"Yeah, scurrying around everywhere like little cockroaches. Right? I mean, we have Clerics back at the Commonwealth. They're just not, like, in charge, you know?"''<br />({{FOTV|2|3}})</ref><ref>[[Xander Harkness|Paladin Xander Harkness]]: ''"You know, back East, it's guys like us that call the shots. People who put their ass on the line. That is foundational to the Brotherhood ethos. Your Clerics? What do they do? Stand around, give speeches. We are the ones who bleed."''<br />({{FOTV|2|3}})</ref> Meanwhile, other chapters such as the [[Yosemite Brotherhood of Steel|Yosemite Brotherhood]] retained a largely secular character.<ref>[[McRae|Yosemite elder]]: ''"The chapters have grown apart over the years, and mine doesn't go in for any of this religious shit."''<br />({{FOTV|2|2}})</ref>

====Eden====
[[File:FOTV Brotherhood lineup.jpg|thumb|Brotherhood knights being blessed by a [[cleric]] prior to a mission]]
Eden refers to a prophesied future created by the Brotherhood of Steel. It was first indirectly spoken about by the organization's founder, [[Roger Maxson|High Elder Roger Maxson]]. His vision for the organization's future would eventually see them use the vast stockpiles of technology they had collected to reclaim and reestablish civilization on the American continent. Maxson saw the Brotherhood's preservation and safeguarding of technology they would need to rebuild the world as crucial. He described the Brotherhood as acting like a hard shell around a precious seed, but would also state that, "one day, when the time is right, that seed will grow. And a new civilization will be born."<ref name="prestech" /><ref name="formation">[[Formation of the Brotherhood of Steel]]</ref> By 2296, the concept of Eden was formally used by the Knights of San Fernando as an ideal for how their chapter can better the world, though their aspirants' knowledge of what the Brotherhood deems as paradise may be limited.<ref>[[Maximus]]: ''"I just want to thank you. For your guidance and for giving me a home. I had no place in the world. So if I can help the Brotherhood make it better. Eden or whatever even if it means giving my own life. I’ll do it. And I’d be grateful to the Brotherhood for giving my life meaning."''<br />{{FOTV|1|1}}</ref>

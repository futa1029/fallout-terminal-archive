'''Vincent May-Lilly''' is a former art teacher and [[Robobrain (Fallout 76)|Robobrain]] found at [[Watoga High School]] in [[Appalachia]] {{in|FO76}}.

==Background==
Vincent May-Lilly is a famous [[Watoga]]n artist and sculptor whose brain has been transplanted into a [[Robobrain (Fallout 76)|Robobrain]]. He taught an art course prior to the [[Great War]] at Watoga High School. Students were to compete for the exclusive twelve slots in his class.<ref>[[Watoga High School terminal entries#New Art Studio|Watoga High School terminal entries; New Art Studio]]</ref> The school's headmaster, [[Reginald Reinhold|Reginald R. Reinhold III]], commented poorly of Vincent's art skills, saying he was a pretentious no-talent hack when he was alive, and as a Robobrain, his art skills are now twice as bad.<ref>[[Watoga High School terminal entries#Memorandum: New art teacher|Watoga High School terminal entries; Memorandum: New art teacher]]</ref>

After the Great War, Vincent became trapped in the high school, unable to escape it for twenty-five years. He has lost the ability to communicate. He has four images posted to his back: feces on a brain, a brain on top of blood with a mushroom cloud behind it, stick figures shooting guns at a bleeding robot, and the text UNPLUG ME.

Prior to the Great War, a [[Todd Nienkerk|student]] is said to have vandalized a Robobrain teacher and was to appear before a student jury.<ref>[[Watoga High School terminal entries#Student Jury|Watoga High School terminal entries; Student Jury]]</ref>

==Behind the scenes==
Vincent was planned for inclusion within the cut [[Magnum Opus]] quest. The cutting of this quest inspired a piece of graffiti seen in-game at [[Bolton Greens]], featuring a mob killing a Robobrain.<ref group="Non-game">BGS_Steve ([[Steve Massey]]) [https://web.archive.org/web/20230513050821/https://i.imgur.com/aIybIKY.png on Bethesda Game Studios' official Discord]</ref> He has [[Vincent May-Lilly/Dialogue|unused dialogue]] which reveals he originally could speak and was going to be voiced by [[Bruce Barker]]. Vincent's unused dialogue shows he was envisioned to be an upbeat character who found the apocalypse a relief from society as he could focus on his art, and that he found the peace and quiet liberating. It is also revealed the nearby paintings and vases were intended to be his. However, in the final game, Vincent was turned into a mute character. Despite his dialogue being removed, an interaction prompt still remains, although it yields nothing from Vincent.

===Death and legacy===
Sometime between October 2281 and July [[Timeline (2250-2300)#2296|2296]], Edward Sallow died from unknown causes. Before his passing, he wrote a note proclaiming his final plans for the Legion: that he did not intend to have a successor and that the Legion was to die with him.<ref name="S2E8">{{FOTV|2|8}}</ref> Caesar kept this note on his body at the time of his death.<ref name="S2E3">{{FOTV|2|3}}</ref>

Before this information could be revealed, rivalries within the Legion, previously kept in check by Sallow's leadership, escalated to the point of fragmenting the once unified army, with at least two different men declaring themselves the new Caesar. The conflict was worsened by a misunderstanding; both sides believed that Sallow had actually used the note to name the person he intended to be his successor. By July 2296, Sallow's decayed skeleton had been left on top of a dirt mound dividing the [[Caesar's Legion camp|central camp]] of the two quarreling Legion factions in the Mojave, still holding the paper announcing his final decision; both sides violently prevent the other from approaching it and revealing its contents to preserve their power.<ref name="S2E3" />

[[File:FOTV S2E8 Caesar's note.png|thumb|242x242px|Caesar's note]]
The civil war was finally ended around that same time after the simmering conflict between the two successor factions was reignited by [[the Ghoul]]'s interference, with at least one of the self-proclaimed successors confirmed to have died, and the other seemingly no longer contesting the throne, if not having also died.<ref>{{FOTV|2|8}}: The Caesar successor (the one played by [[Kaleti Williams]]) is seen dead during the scene when the Lacerta Legate takes Edward Sallow's remains into his tent. The other successor does not appear, but he evidently is no longer a factor given that the Lacerta Legate was accepted by all of the surviving legionaries as their new Caesar.</ref> Amid the conflict, the [[Lacerta Legate]], the second-in-command of one of the two self-proclaimed successors, recovered Edward Sallow's remains and read his final note: "I AM CAESAR. I AM THE LEGION. IT ENDS WITH ME."  

The Lacerta Legate, discovering Sallow's actual intentions for the Legion, refused to accept them. He chose to hide this information, disposing of the note by eating it, murdering the [[Stabbed legionary|one legionary]] who saw him read it, and taking Edward Sallow's crown to declare himself as the new Caesar.<ref name="S2E8" /> The newly-crowned Caesar gathered the surviving Legion forces to march on New Vegas with the intent of seizing it as their holy capital city, just as Edward Sallow had sought to since two decades earlier.<ref>[[Lacerta Legate]]: ''"Together, we will reclaim the holy land that they call Vegas. ===Philosophy===
{{Quotation|position=right|A case can be made that Caesar should have an evil (or very evil) alignment, but he also exists in a bubble that insulates him from what he's doing. Caesar does what he does because he feels that it is contributing to a better society|Joshua Sawyer on Caesar's alignment}}
The bedrock of Caesar's philosophy is that service to the state is not just the highest virtue, it is the ''only'' virtue. Caesar is critical of NCR society as its leaders care not for the "greater good". This is contrary to Caesar's belief that "an individual has no value beyond his utility to the state, whether as an instrument of war, or production." He views the [[Great War]] of 2077 as a chance for humanity to start over and aims to unify humanity under one banner or eliminate them should they refuse. Reading through old world books, Caesar has gained knowledge of the old glory of Imperial Rome and has used the ignorance of his followers to claim that he is Caesar, the son of Mars, the God of War, and not Edward Sallow, former Follower of the Apocalypse.

Aside from total military defeat, Caesar's greatest fear is being exposed as a fraud. Most members of the Legion, excluding some of the most trusted ones, are oblivious to the fact that the Legion's culture is based on books about ancient Rome – they believe that all of the customs enforced by Caesar were dictated to him by Mars himself. Those who claim otherwise are viewed as ultimate blasphemes and are likely to be executed.

====Killing Caesar====
If the player character manages to kill Caesar, the effects on the game are fairly minor, although the Legion ending is largely changed, due to the fact that Caesar, as the leader of the Legion, no longer directly participates in the events.

* If the Courier has the [[Terrifying Presence (Fallout: New Vegas)|Terrifying Presence]] perk and a negative reputation with the Legion prior to meeting Caesar, he will ask what they were thinking when first meeting him, and the option to tell him "That I'd decorate this tent with your guts." can be used to frighten him before attacking.
* If Caesar dies for any reason other than his illness or using the autodoc to dissect his brain then passing a speech check to pacify [[Lucius]] in [[Et Tumor, Brute?]], Legion [[Fallout: New Vegas reputations|reputation]] will automatically change to Vilified, even if the player character didn't kill him.
* [[Julie Farkas]] exclaims "So, the great Caesar is dead ..." and goes on saying that she doubts that it will have much effect on the imminent attack.
* If the player character talks to Mr. House about killing Caesar before he dies, Mr. House will reply that "[He] does not want you to touch one hair on that man's head, assuming you can find one." He says that Caesar provides a good distraction for the NCR.
** If the player character kills Caesar while working with Mr. House on the "The House Always Wins" quests, they can return to Mr. House and tell him that Caesar is dead. Mr. House simply notes that this has a "minimal" impact on the [[Second Battle of Hoover Dam|battle for Hoover Dam]] and offers no reward or punishment for the action.<ref>The [[Courier]]: ''"You do know Caesar is dead, don't you?"''<br />[[Robert House]]: ''"By my calculations, his death will affect the shape of the battle for Hoover Dam minimally, if at all. The Legion's aggression will outlive Caesar. Indeed, they'll try to take the dam as a tribute to his memory. Given a year, they'd have him deified - but by then the Legion will be breaking down, riven by internal conflicts, a monster consuming itself. It's irrelevant. In the short term, the Legion is still monster enough that defeating it will make me look powerful indeed."''<br />([[MrHouse.txt|Robert House's dialogue]])</ref>
* If the player character kills Caesar, [[Mr. New Vegas]] will say "it is still unknown how the assassin managed to evade security," even if they killed Caesar in a frontal assault on The Fort (i.e., didn't evade security).
* If Caesar is killed with Boone as a companion, Boone will smugly say "Thumbs down, you son of a bitch!" - a reference to the [[Wikipedia:Pollice verso|gesture]] that is commonly thought to have called for the execution of a failed gladiator in ancient Rome, despite the thumb inside the fist meaning "mercy" or "weapons down." He will subsequently have further dialogue options when selecting the "Talk" option. He will also talk about the death of Caesar having little effect on the attack on Hoover Dam, but admits, on prompting from the Courier, that he still enjoyed it immensely. They will also gain two points to use toward Boone's quest [[I Forgot to Remember to Forget]].
* If the player character speaks to [[Lily Bowen|Lily]], she will remark that Leo thinks there will be more people to chop up and that the player character should be careful.
*There is a new dialogue option when the player confronts Legate Lanius, where the player can ask Lanius, "So, Caesar giving orders from beyond the grave?" - an indication that Mr. House is right and Caesar's death was no more than a minimal setback. However, House will also note that Lanius is a poor leader compared to Caesar, and predicts that within a year of his death the Legion will be torn apart by infighting.
* [[Caleb McCaffery]] greets the Courier with "All hail the slayer of Caesar!"
* Most [[NCR trooper (Fallout: New Vegas)|NCR trooper]]s will say, "Wish I could have been there to see Caesar die. What an asshole."
* [[Chairmen]] at [[The Tops]] will state "That's the guy/gal who killed Caesar!"
* On visiting the [[Atomic Wrangler Casino|Atomic Wrangler]], both [[James Garret|James]] and [[Francine Garret]] will offer the player free bottles of liquor, with Francine stating that it "serves him right for treating women like livestock."
* Some travelers on the Strip will comment, "Now that you've killed that Caesar bastard, the Strip's really going to bloom."
* [[White Glove]]s in the [[Ultra-Luxe]] will comment, "I hear you killed Caesar, is it true, that he wore a toupee?"
* [[McCredie|Sergeant McCredie]] at [[Camp Golf]] will complement the Courier on a great job, and [[Mags]] will say "I hear Caesar's dead, and we have you to thank for it... nice work."
* While near the end of [[Return to Sender]], the player character can use Caesar's death to convince [[Hanlon|Chief Hanlon]] to come back to his senses and cease falsifying reports.
* If the player kills Caesar before starting ''[[Honest Hearts]]'', it is possible to inform [[Joshua Graham]] of Caesar's death. He will not react much to the news but he will admit that he thought he would die before Caesar. He also remarks that Caesar's death is good news for the Mojave, and states that without Caesar's leadership the Legion will eventually fall apart. However, the Courier cannot do the reverse, as killing Caesar's former righthand man (or even leaving him alive) brings no dialogue options with him.
* If the player character speaks to [[Ulysses]] after the end of ''[[Lonesome Road (add-on)|Lonesome Road]]'' they can tell him that they have killed Caesar. He isn't angry despite his former allegiance to the Legion, viewing Caesar as "history's hypocrite,"<ref>Courier: ''"Are you angry at all?"''<br /> Ulysses: ''"Caesar would be history's hypocrite if he was angry about what happened. I'm not one for it, either. Seems you just proved his philosophy to him. If he'd been stronger, he'd be alive, and we wouldn't be talking."''</ref> and states that the east may fall apart in time but that it's too soon to tell; he goes on to say that unless they do the same to [[Lanius]], they'll get no thanks from him.

===Endings===
* In the Legion ending, if the player found a way to cure Caesar's brain tumor, Caesar becomes the new ruler of Vegas after the Legion conquers the city. He imposes his harsh and unforgiving vision of order upon Vegas, enslaving much of the population. If the player failed to cure him, Legate Lanius is crowned as the new Caesar instead.
* In all other endings, Caesar's fate is left unspecified.

* Most members of the Legion pronounce Caesar's name /ˈkaizar/, a classical Latin pronunciation emphasizing the [[Wikipedia:Æ#Latin|diphthong]], because that was the way they learned it from Caesar's teachings. Some ex-Legion members, like [[Joshua Graham]], as well as most other wastelanders, pronounce it as the Anglicized /ˈsiːzər/.<ref group="Non-game">Question: ''"Ok, really, what's the deal with some people pronouncing Ceasar's legion "Kaesar" and other people pronouncing it "Seesar" why does this happen, and what's the proper pronounciation?"''<br />[[Joshua Sawyer]]: ''"Most Legion folk pronounce it using classical Latin pronunciation (kai-zar) because that is how they learned the name from Caesar. * If Caesar enters combat, he may say, "For the Legion!" However, the subtitle reads, "For Caesar!"
* If Caesar dies, his body will never disappear.
* If Caesar turns hostile, he appears to not have an "attacking expression," and will attack with an expression on his face as if he wasn't angry.

==Behind the scenes==
* [[John Gonzalez]] wrote Caesar and [[Joshua Sawyer]] asked for Caesar to present his rationale in the framework of his interpretation of Hegelian dialectics.<ref group="Non-game">[https://archive.is/kZfc1 so who did the writing for Caesar in New Vegas, and who decided he should give an overview of Hegelian dialectics? Formspring]</ref>
* If the Courier speaks to Caesar before attacking him, they can say "Death to tyrants!" This is a common mistranslation of the Latin phrase "''[[wikipedia:Sic semper tyrannis|Sic semper tyrannis]]''." The actual translation of this phrase is "Thus always to tyrants." This quote is attributed to the assassins of the real Julius Caesar, though the veracity is unclear.
** The phrase is also the name of [[Sic Semper Tyrannis|a challenge]] which requires assassinating the NCR's president [[Aaron Kimball]]. In this context, the quote is a reference to the [[wikipedia:Assassination of Abraham Lincoln|assassination of Abraham Lincoln]], as Lincoln's killer, [[John Wilkes Booth]], is traditionally said to have quoted the phrase during his escape; Booth also wrote that he used the phrase because of its association with Julius Caesar's assassination.
* Caesar possesses a number of parallels to his historical counterpart. For example, the historical Caesar actually suffered from a [[Wikipedia:Julius Caesar#Health and physical appearance|condition]] similar to the one depicted in-game. He had headaches, blackouts, and sometimes even seizures. In terms of appearance, Caesar is balding and has an [[Wikipedia:Aquiline nose|Aquiline nose]], a sign of nobility in ancient Rome. One thing of note is that Caesar is 55 years old in 2281, and the historical Julius Caesar was assassinated at the age of 56 on March 15, 44 B.C.
** Caesar claims to be the Son of Mars, which is a trait based on the historical Julius Caesar, who similarly claimed to be descended from the god Mars.
** In a quote, Caesar refers to the [[Rubicon|Rubicon River]], which the real [[Wikipedia:Gaius Julius Caesar|Julius Caesar]] actually crossed in 49 B.C., triggering the [[Wikipedia:Roman civil wars|Roman civil wars]]. Roman armies were forbidden from crossing it (and, as such, entering Rome itself) except for few exceptions. Therefore, having his armies cross the river was the same as a declaration of war and as such an irreversible decision, a point of no return. This reference, in combination with Caesar's plan of making Vegas the true capital of his empire, parallels the historical Caesar.
** Caesar's lack of an intended successor for the Legion by the time of his death, as seen in the ''Fallout'' TV series, may also be a reference to the historical Julius Caesar: as [[Joshua Sawyer]] notes, the historical Julius Caesar did not leave a "clear line of succession," which resulted in a civil war, the [[wikipedia:War of Actium|War of Actium]], over his succession.<ref group="Non-game">[[Joshua Sawyer]]: ''"RE: Caesar's plans: yes, he views the Colorado River as the Rubicon and the NCR as the corrupt Roman Republic waiting to be torn down via military occupation. This is literally exactly what Julius Caesar did with Legio XIII Gemina. Other things Julius Caesar did:

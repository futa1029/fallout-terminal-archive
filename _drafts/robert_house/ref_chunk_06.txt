use's ideology]]}}

{{Quotation|[Question: ''How would you describe Mr. House's Ideology? I know that he's rather authoritarian when it comes to political freedoms, but what about on economic issues? He was my favorite character in the game, and whoever wrote him did an outstanding job.'']<br />He's also authoritarian on economic issues within certain boundaries. In the same way that a fascist government would exert authority on the various production/capital resources involved in a market, Mr. House exerts his authority on The Strip's operation. Ultimately, he wants The Strip to be (extremely) profitable and to retain independence from NCR/CL. However, he does not want the families to be independent from his authority, so he does what he can to keep them under his thumb. He doesn't want to micromanage what they do, but he also knows that allowing them to do whatever they want could eventually lead to the downfall of his authority or fighting between the families (which would also undermine his authority).<br />Really, the main area in which Mr. House is "liberal" is in personal consumption and behavior. As long as it makes him money and doesn't create instability or feuding on The Strip, he allows it.|[[Joshua Sawyer]]'s [[Joshua Sawyer Formspring posts/2012|Formspring on Robert House's ideology]]}}

====Developer context====
* Mr. House was created and written by [[John Gonzalez]].<ref group="Meta">[https://web.archive.org/web/20221217180022/https://twitter.com/jesawyer/status/1604171903978962944 Joshua Sawyer on Twitter]: ''"He created and wrote House, designed the families of The Strip, and wrote many major characters, including House, Caesar, Benny, Yes Man, and Vulpes Inculta. He also wrote Ricky and Stella in Honest Hearts as well as all of the Survivalist/Randall Clark logs. 3/8"''</ref><ref name="Hughes" group="Meta">Question: ''"Was Ayn Rand by any chance part of the inspiration for Mr. House? He seems very much like her type of heroic businessman and laissez-faire capitalist."''<br />[[Joshua Sawyer]]: ''"He was most heavily inspired by Howard Hughes. John Gonzalez created and wrote all of Mr. House's dialogue, so he might be able to offer more detailed insight."''<br />([[Joshua Sawyer Formspring posts/2011]])</ref>
* Character artist [[Kevin Manning]] worked on modeling for Mr. House, though he did not specify whether this referred to his computer screen, his physical body, or both.<ref group="Meta">[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hRWoX165zik Memories of the Mojave With Kevin Manning]</ref> House's physical body was based on a concept by [[Brian Menze]] (who also painted House's main portrait), and according to area designer [[J.R. Vosovic]], it was likely Manning who modeled it. Vosovic worked with animator [[Shon Stewart]] to connect House's animations to the lower chamber in which he is found.<ref group="Meta">[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q54zXSAkfgk The New Vegas Strip's Development & Cut Content]</ref>
* [[Joshua Sawyer]] stated that the platinum chip was carried by the Courier from southern NCR through [[the Hub]], at Mr. House's behest.<ref group="Meta">[https://archive.is/9WDRV J.E. Sawyer]: "The Courier carried his/her delivery from southern NCR, toward the Hub. We intentionally left the Courier's origins and the exact nature of the initial transaction vague."</ref>

====Historical and political context====
* Robert House is based on [[wikipedia:Howard Hughes|Howard Hughes]], who was similarly a reclusive millionaire with ties to the real world [[wikipedia:Las Vegas Strip|Las Vegas Strip]].<ref name="Hughes" group="Meta" /> Hughes had a habit of raising his left eyebrow in photographs, which shows on House's monitor portrait. The [[House Resort]] contains a large portrait of Mr. House standing in front of an enormous, bipedal robot; the portrait is based on a real-world photograph of Howard Hughes standing in front of a Boeing 100A aircraft in 1934.<ref group="Meta">[https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/49/Howard_Hughes.jpg Hughes Boeing 100A photo]</ref>
** Jane, House's Securitron companion, is a reference to real world actress [[Wikipedia:Jane Russell|Jane Russell]], who worked for Hughes' production company for several years.
** Like Hughes, House used a body double for his public facing persona before the Great War.
* When asked about House's political ideology, [[Chris Avellone]] deferred a more definitive answer to [[John Gonzalez]], as he was the main writer for House. However, Avellone did say that while he "wasn't aware of any [particular] ideology, [the developers] didn't usually lean on any specific real world ideologies when crafting characters - although [people] have interpreted them as such."<ref group="Meta">[https://archive.is/IyVnp Chris Avellone on Twitter]: ''"John Gonzalez (lead writer) might be the best one to ask, as he wrote him. While I wasn't aware of any particlar{{sic}} ideology, we didn't usually lean on any specific real world ideologies when crafting characters - although ppl have interpreted them as such."''</ref> However, in the "[[Behind the Bright Lights & Big City]]" section of the collector's edition ''[[Fallout: New Vegas Official Game Guide]]'', House is described as a "libertarian dictator."<ref group="Supplement">''"So long as Mr. House's basic rules are followed, his intentions are to take a "hands off" approach towards the subjects of his kingdom; he is effectively a libertarian dictator creating his own version of paradise rooted in the old world. If the player supports Mr. House, she is supporting the New Vegas libertopia, a place where the strong rule unfettered for as long as they stay strong and where the weak are continually trampled underfoot. But it's a place where Mr. House wants you to dream, that someday, you could be on of the strong."''<br />([[Behind the Bright Lights & Big City]], ''[[Fallout: New Vegas Official Game Guide]]'' collector's edition, p. 463</ref>

====Cut content====

* Mr. House's computer screen originally had a different portrait displayed on it, featuring more of his body, facing slightly off-center, and with a somewhat different face (see [[#Gallery|below]]). According to [[Joshua Sawyer]], this portrait was replaced relatively late in development because he felt it made House look too "manic."<ref group="Meta">Question: ''"Exactly when in development did the team decide to change the portrait for Mr. House, and why? Was the previous one just something of a placeholder?"''<br />[[Joshua Sawyer]]: ''"It was relatively late in development. I felt the original portrait made Mr. House look too manic."''<br/ >([[Joshua Sawyer Formspring posts|Formspring reply from September 16, 2011]])</ref>
* In addition to Jane, Mr. House originally had another female programmed Securitron companion named [[Marilyn (Fallout: New Vegas)|Marilyn]] who would have been found in the [[Lucky 38 presidential suite]]. As with Jane being named after real-world actress [[wikipedia:Jane Russell|Jane Russell]], Marilyn was named after [[Marilyn Monroe]] as a reference to both women being famous contemporaries. According to Joshua Sawyer, Marilyn was cut only because of voice acting issues late in development.<ref group="Meta">Question: ''"Why was Marilyn cut? Since she is featured on the cards and Veronica alludes to her, I'm guessing she was cut rather late in the development process?"''<br />[[Joshua Sawyer]]: ''"Apparently, there were some errors with her VO that weren't able to be fixed, so she was cut."''<br/ >([[Joshua Sawyer Formspring posts|Formspring reply from October 23, 2011]])</ref>
* The ''Fallout: New Vegas'' game files indicate that there was originally an option for a female Courier to "seduce" Mr. House. This likely ties in with Raul's dialogue if you ask him about Mr House. He says House used to "scan women's brains" and "make them dress up in different clothes" in lieu of actual err, sexual acts - this is probably exactly what would have happened in this sequence.<br />There is also a cut VR pod called L38PlayerPod that contains seduction stuff. * There is a cut quest option in [[The House Always Wins V]] where the Courier would have been able to convince House to spare the Mojave Brotherhood of Steel and compromise by leaving them alone. ===''Fallout'' TV series===
* {{Note|(Note 2)}} In the ''Fallout'' TV series, Robert House is played by actor [[Justin Theroux]], starting from the show's second season. This was explicitly confirmed in promotional materials and interviews for the show around the time of season 2's release.<ref name="Gizmodo Teaser" group="Meta">[https://archive.ph/eRVrF Gizmodo: "In 'Fallout' Season 2's First Trailer, All Roads Lead to New Vegas" (archived)]</ref> Meanwhile, [[Rafi Silver]] plays Mr. House's body double, who first appeared in the season 1 finale "[[The Beginning]]." However, within the show itself, Silver's character was credited as Robert House both in "The Beginning" and in the first episode of season 2, "[[The Innovator]];" he was first credited as the Robert House double in "[[The Wrangler]]." Theroux's character remains unnamed in the credits of all of the season 2 episodes he appears in, though the show itself confirms he is the actual Robert House.
** In an interview from December 2025, Geneva Robertson-Dworet and Justin Theroux alluded to the relationship between Theroux's House and Silver's House body double. Robertson-Dworet commented that this was based on Howard Hughes' increasing reclusiveness near the end of his life, stating that the writers wanted to depict House as someone "constantly playing tricks on the world" and "maybe somewhat scared to go out because, think of what a targeted man he is." Theroux suggested that House could have "become so paranoid, or so rarefied, that he needs to live above Las Vegas in a very protected place," also noting that the setting of the late 2070s made it "still possible to have avatars in the world where people wouldn't recognize [House]."<ref name="SR" group="Meta" />

Focused only on achieving his goals, he has little interest in monitoring or controlling the lives of others. He allows autonomy to the Three Families and is largely reliant on the information networks at his disposal and his Securitron patrols to maintain order. He takes pride in his achievements, seeing himself as a visionary. He also takes great interest in the Courier, seeing them as a valuable asset for overcoming obstacles too challenging for his other agents. Though their relationship is mainly professional, Mr. House comes to regard the Courier with respect and shows appreciation for their services, should they choose to help Mr. House. ===Quests===
* [[The House Always Wins]]: Mr. House gives the player character the quest, which leads to one of the final quests, [[All or Nothing]]. In this case, the player character communicates with House only through the computer terminal in his office.
* [[For the Republic, Part 2]], [[Wild Card: Change in Management]], [[Render Unto Caesar]]: However, if the player character decides to side with NCR, Caesar or fight for an independent New Vegas, Mr. House has to be killed or disabled.
* [[The Moon Comes Over the Tower]]: [[Emily Ortal]] asks the player character to bug one of House's terminals for important medical information.

===Effects of player's actions===
* Upon his death, the quest [[The House Has Gone Bust!]] will simultaneously trigger and fail, and his obituary "[[A tragedy has befallen all mankind|A Tragedy Has Befallen All Mankind]]" will appear in the player character's inventory.
* If the player character takes Mr. House out of his stasis chamber, he will ask them why they have ruined his plans and he will react differently depending on what they tell him. 
** If told they did it in the name of the [[New California Republic|NCR]], he will belittle them for working for them, declaring them "snakes," and call the Courier a "sad, misguided whore."
** If told they are acting on behalf of [[Caesar]], he will be horrified at the prospect of slavery being humanity's future.
** If told they did it for [[Yes Man]], he will tell them their "vanity project" is doomed for failure. 
** If saying it was "just business," he will retort by saying that they should have worked for him if what they wanted was personal gain.
** Finally, if the player character says they did it just because they didn't like him, he will call them a fool for letting their feelings about him jeopardize humanity's future.

===Other interactions===
* Mr. House plays a major role in the game. The Courier hears a lot about him while traveling, but upon reaching New Vegas, they are invited via Victor to visit House in Lucky 38. There, he provides vital information about [[Benny]] and the [[platinum chip]] and allows the Courier and their companions to use the [[Lucky 38 presidential suite]] as housing.
* Mr. House has an interest in the collectible [[Snow globe (Fallout: New Vegas)|snow globes]] found in the game, and will pay handsomely for each.<ref>The [[Courier]]: ''"What's the deal with the snowglobe collection?"''<br />Robert House: ''"What of it? I enjoy them. There's something about a little diorama set inside a glass dome that I... {{Tooltip|find|beat}} pleasing. If you run across any out in the wastes, turn them in to Jane. She'll compensate you."''<br />([[MrHouse.txt|Robert House's dialogue]])</ref> The snow globes can be given to [[Jane (Lucky 38)|Jane]] in exchange for 2000 [[Bottle cap (Fallout: New Vegas)|cap]]s each. Snow globes found in [[Snow globe - Sierra Madre|Sierra Madre]] (''[[Dead Money]]''), [[Snow globe - Big MT|Big MT]] (''[[Old World Blues (add-on)|Old World Blues]]''), [[Snow globe - Zion National Park|Zion National Park]] (''[[Honest Hearts]]'') and [[the Divide]] (''[[Lonesome Road (add-on)|Lonesome Road]]'') will automatically be removed from the player's inventory and replaced with 2000 caps (with the exception of the Sierra Madre snow globe, which adds 2000 [[Sierra Madre chip]]s). Once the [[Courier]] has sold a snow globe to Jane it is placed on display (on a mantle) in the Lucky 38 presidential suite. However, if the player kills Mr. House, the snow globes will stay in their inventory and Jane will disappear.
* Mr. House will have unique dialogue if one sides with him and then completes [[Beyond the Beef]] by either stopping Mortimer's plans (he approves of the player's actions)<ref>The [[Courier]]: ''"Did you know I killed some White Gloves who were eating their customers?"''<br>[[Robert House|Mr. House]]: ''"Cannibalism was strictly forbidden in their contract. If some of them broke that stricture, they deserved whatever punishment you dealt them."''<br>([[MrHouse.txt|Mr. House's dialogue]])</ref> or helping him return the Society to cannibalism (he authorizes the player to deal with them however for breaking their contract).<ref>The [[Courier]]: ''"Did you know some of the White Gloves are... eating people again?"''<br>[[Robert House|Mr. House]]: ''{{Tooltip|"No, I was not aware of this.|Perturbed by the news}} Is it really so hard {{Tooltip|not|Emphasized}} to engage in cannibalism? My goodness... It's a violation of their contract. I authorize you to deal with them in any way you see fit."''<br>([[MrHouse.txt|Mr. House's dialogue]])</ref>
* The Courier can also attempt to pickpocket Mr. House, but he does not yield any items. 
* Mr. House counts as an abomination for the [[Abominable]] challenge. Because he is considered an abomination, shooting him with the [[Flare gun (Lonesome Road)|flare gun]] results in the "The abomination panics and flees!" message, but nothing else happens.
* House is one of the characters that the player character must eat in order to earn the [[Meat of Champions]] perk.

===[[The Strip]]===
House, newly restored, expresses pleasure at being alive again after his physical body expired and reveals to the Ghoul that over the years since he copied his mind to the computer, his body had become something of a target for wandering travelers with something to prove, resulting in House being poisoned, shot and bludgeoned with a crowbar, resulting in House's physical death. The Ghoul threatens to shoot the diode which House admits will have unknown effects but urges the Ghoul to exercise extreme caution as the damage would likely extend to other planets. The Ghoul questions why House hadn't told him about the management vault in Vegas which House reveals was because the Ghoul hadn't brought him the diode yet. The two men reach a deal where House will lead the Ghoul to his family in exchange for House being left the cold fusion diode and House smugly tells the Ghoul that like it or not, everyone eventually ends up working for him.

Inhabiting a [[Pip-Boy]], House monologues about how Vault-Tec had wanted an impenetrable Vault buried half a mile below the city, but he had always been one step ahead, rigging every part of the city to protect its future and even rigging the Vault for management. However, House had miscalculated because the facility belongs not to Vault-Tec, but their investors. House was off by a bit when it came to predicting the end of the world, but he was wholly outclassed when it came to controlling the future by the invisible adversary who set the [[Deathclaw (TV series)|Deathclaws]] on [[New Vegas]] and to whom the Ghoul had delivered the cold fusion diode two hundred years ago: the [[Enclave]], whom [[Hank MacLean]] is currently working for. In doing so, the Ghoul had helped bring forth the greatest threat to the Wasteland. House eventually helps the Ghoul find his wife and daughter's cryo pods, but they're empty with only a postcard from [[Barb Howard]] left behind. Having found proof that his family is still alive, the Ghoul abandons the Pip-Boy and House on the floor while he goes to search for Barb and Janey.

[[Maximus]] and [[Lucy MacLean]] enter the Lucky 38 penthouse soon after the Ghoul's departure; they do not see House's face on the monitor, which only displays a Signal Lost message. As Max and Lucy look out at [[Caesar's Legion]] marching into New Vegas, House briefly appears on the monitor, unnoticed by the pair.

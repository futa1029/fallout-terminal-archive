
* Prior to the release of season 2, Theroux's appearance as House was spotlighted in a ''GQ'' article. According to [[Geneva Robertson-Dworet]] and [[Jonathan Nolan]], the showrunners behind the TV series were interested in exploring House as a character, especially prior to the Great War. This interest was due to House himself being synonymous with Vegas, and they had already decided on going there as the primary setting of season 2. Additionally, they saw House's character as being relevant in the real world in 2025, at a time when "very wealthy technocrats have undue influence in the world," according to Nolan.<ref name="GQ" group="Meta">[https://archive.ph/ZG0ma GQ: "First Look at Justin Theroux in ''Fallout'' Season 2: Inside the Show's Big Trip to Vegas" (archived)]</ref>
** Theroux was convinced to join the cast of ''Fallout''{{'}}s second season by [[Walton Goggins]], who is a close friend of Theroux. Theroux was already admired as an actor by Jonathan Nolan, who suggested to Goggins that he make the pitch. Prior to the offer, Theroux had neither seen the ''Fallout'' show nor played a ''Fallout'' game, but he accepted the offer and studied House's character by watching gameplay videos of ''Fallout: New Vegas'' on YouTube. According to Theroux, he believed Nolan saw real-world parallels in the present to the type of character House was, while Theroux himself personally interpreted House's character as being similar to [[wikipedia:Robert McNamara|Robert McNamara]], the real-world Secretary of Defense under [[wikipedia:John F. Kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] and [[wikipedia:Lyndon B. Johnson|Lyndon B. Johnson]], especially in relation to his prediction of nuclear threat at the height of the [[Cold War]]. Theroux also mentioned Howard Hughes as a reference point for his performance,<ref name="GQ" group="Meta" /> reflecting how Hughes was cited as a significant influence for House's character as played by [[René Auberjonois]] during the original development of ''Fallout: New Vegas''.
** As other inspirations, Theroux also cited studying "radio announcers, newsmen and people who had [House's] specific dialect."<ref name="SR" group="Meta">[https://archive.ph/g9WiA Screen Rant: "Fallout Season 2: Justin Theroux On Taking Over Iconic Mr. House Role" (archived)]</ref>
* After the release of "The Handoff," [[Geneva Robertson-Dworet]] said in an interview that a representation of Robert House powered by cold fusion seemed like the "ultimate version" of House. She explained that the writers viewed this as something it made sense for House, as a person interested in robotics and self-preservation, to develop, and that it also allowed the writers to include House in the story without overruling players' gameplay experiences in ''Fallout: New Vegas''. However, she declined to state whether this would be the "''final'' final form" of House in the series.<ref name="GRD house" group="Meta" />
* In a GQ interview following the release of "The Strip," Walton Goggins was asked how the scene between the Ghoul and Robert House's avatar in the Lucky 38 penthouse during "The Strip" was filmed. Goggins revealed that both he and Justin Theroux were fitted with 35mm camera rigs, with Theroux being projected in full costume onto the screen in real-time so both actors could play off of each other and convey their characters' relationship; Goggins agreed with the interviewer's comparison of the situation to a Zoom call.<ref group="Meta">[https://www.gq.com/story/walton-goggins-fallout-season-2-finale-interview 'Fallout' Star Walton Goggins Says the Season 2 Finale Was All About 'Hope' | GQ: (February 5, 2026)]</ref>

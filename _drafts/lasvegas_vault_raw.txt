The '''Las Vegas management Vault'''{{Ref|(Note 1)}} is a [[Fallout TV series locations|location]] in [[Fallout TV series season 2|season 2]] of the [[Fallout (TV series)|''Fallout'' TV series]].

==Background==
This facility was one of a series of special vaults for management employees of the [[Vault-Tec Corporation]] to oversee the other mainline Vaults.<ref>[[Barb Howard]]: ''"I have worked hard to make sure we go into a special vault for management, where we will oversee all of the other vaults."''<br />({{FOTV|1|6}})</ref> It was one of many such facilities, along with one built somewhere in [[Oregon]] and another somewhere in [[California]].<ref name="management">[[Lucy MacLean]]: ''"I'd ask why my dad ran into a den of giant monsters, but you'd probably just tell me to shut the fudge up."''<br />[[The Ghoul]]: ''"[sighs] Before the war, there was a plan... for a special Vault. A Vault for management. That's where my wife is. Where my daughter is, too."''<br />Lucy MacLean: ''"You never found it?"''<br />The Ghoul: ''"Oh no, I found one, in California. It was empty. Found another one in Oregon. It was empty, too. Turns out, there's more than a few special Vaults for management sprinkled across the Wasteland. All of 'em empty. But I've never found one in Vegas."''<br />Lucy MacLean: ''"So, you think if my dad's in there, maybe your wife and daughter are, too?"''<br />The Ghoul: ''"Uh-huh. And now, they're surrounded by these fuckin' things."''<br />({{FOTV|2|5}})</ref> While officially a Vault-Tec facility, it was secretly constructed for their financiers, the [[Enclave]]. 

The facility was built half a mile underneath [[New Vegas|Las Vegas]], [[Nevada]], accessible from the [[Lucky 38]] via a series of subterranean tunnels. [[Robert House]], who was aware that nuclear war was inevitable and took steps to preserve Las Vegas from the bombs, permitted Vault-Tec to build this vault, but then "rigged" it in some way to make sure the company could not use it for their plans.<ref name="outclassed">[[Robert House]]: ''"Vault-Tec wanted an impenetrable Vault buried half a mile below the city, but I was always one step ahead."''<br />[[The Ghoul]]: ''"Uh-huh."''<br />House: ''"I rigged every aspect of the city so I could protect its future. I rigged every slot machine, every roulette wheel. Turn left. I even rigged their impenetrable Vault for management."''<br />The Ghoul: ''"Is that so?"''<br />House: ''"But I miscalculated. Because this Vault belongs not to Vault-Tec but to their investors. I never fathomed there could be someone one step ahead of me. Until I saw your demon in the snow. [...] When it came to predicting the end of the world, I was off by just a smidge. But when it came to controlling the future, I was wholly outclassed by an invisible adversary. It was they who set the Deathclaws on Vegas. And now one of their acolytes is here, in this very building. [...] The Enclave's eyes are everywhere. You should know, Mr. Howard. Two hundred years ago, you delivered that diode into their possession yourself. You helped bring forth the greatest threat to the Wasteland. You were their unwitting servant."''<br />({{FOTV|2|8}}) {{Small|Robert House confirms that the deathclaw in Alaska as seen in "The Demon in the Snow" was released by the Enclave.}}</ref> It is not specified what House did, but he is shown to have control over at least some of the management Vault's systems through his mainframe.<ref>{{FOTV|2|8}}: Robert House is able to guide the Ghoul directly into the management Vault and also opens his wife's and daughter's cryo pods for him, indicating that he is familiar with the Vault and its systems.</ref>

At the Las Vegas management vault, research was undertaken on the [[brain-computer interface chip|Black Box chip]] technology developed by House, which he had licensed to Vault-Tec in exchange for ownership of their [[Cold fusion|cold fusion diode]].<ref>{{FOTV|2|6}}</ref> The management Vault's researchers specifically worked on miniaturizing the chip's hardware while maintaining its functionality,<ref>[[Hank MacLean]]: ''"Uh, I've been keeping busy. I'm in Vegas these days, back in your old stomping grounds, actually… trying to pick up where you all left off. Looks like a couple of the Vault experiments ran into some speed bumps. 24 made progress on the brain-computer interface. This one, miniaturization. What it all needs is integrating. But I'm confident that if I just roll up my sleeves, I can bring it all home for you. Then, we can finally talk about my promotion. [blows sharply] I'm gonna complete the work you started. And when this is all over, you will be begging me to help you."''<br />({{FOTV|2|1}})</ref> with experiments on live test subjects including both mice and humans. However, they evidently were not able to overcome the technology's flaw of causing subjects' heads to overload and explode when receiving more complex commands.

Other areas of the facility included a communications room equipped with ham radio technology, as well as three separate sections containing [[cryonics]] facilities: one suite specifically for Vault-Tec management staff, and the "Premium Elite Plus" suite for private individuals (though the cost of a single ticket was noted to be significantly expensive) to be kept preserved until [[Reclamation Day (event)|Reclamation Day]]. [[Barb Howard]], a Vault-Tec senior executive, had a spot within the management cryonics bay, as did her daughter [[Janey Howard|Janey]]. The third preserved animal test subjects.

At least one of these cryo pods was put to use by the time that the [[Great War]] struck in October 2077, with their occupants remaining preserved for the centuries that followed. However, the facility itself was left abandoned, and had remained that way for a very long time by 2296, with only a few of the Black Box chip test subjects' remains still present as signs of former life. At some point, Barb Howard's cryo pod was opened, and a postcard was left inside, seemingly a message from Barb herself to her husband as a clue for where to find them after the war.

===Use by Hank MacLean===
After his escape from [[Griffith Observatory]] in the midst of [[Battle of Griffith Observatory|the attack]] by the [[Knights of San Fernando]] against [[Lee Moldaver]]'s [[New California Republic|NCR]] remnant group,<ref>{{FOTV|1|8}}</ref> disgraced [[Vault 33]] overseer [[Hank MacLean]] fled [[New California]] on a long trek wearing a stolen set of Brotherhood [[T-60 power armor (TV series)|T-60 power armor]] in order to reach the facility, located underneath the [[Lucky 38]] casino in the ruins of [[New Vegas]]. Before getting there, he made a detour to [[Vault 24]] to acquire the hard drive containing the Vault's research data regarding their experiments to enhance the black box chip, bringing it with him for the remainder of his trek. Once there he continued the experiments in mind-control technology, exhausting the animal test-subjects, and later delving into humans left in their cryo-pods before abducting wastelanders before he could perfect the technology. He planned to wipe out the factions warring on the surface like the NCR and Legion, though he was ultimately thwarted by his daughter Lucy. However he had dispatched mind-controlled agents to enact portions of his plan that he kept secret before erasing his own memories. 

==In the TV series==
===[[The Innovator]]===
Around the same time that Hank's daughter [[Lucy MacLean|Lucy]] and [[the Ghoul]] had followed his trail to Vault 24, Hank himself made it into the subterranean Vault-Tec facility. In a room with many desks, he starts up a [[terminal]] and logs into the system, confirming he still retained access to the network. After cleaning up and picking out new work attire, Hank locates the "Global Comms" room and uses the radio console to reach out to an unknown recipient. He says to them that he intends to continue the facility staff's experiments with miniaturizing the chip and combining it with Vault 24's data to perfect the design, all in an effort to curry a "promotion" that he believes will have them "begging [him] to help."

===[[The Golden Rule]]===
Once he locates the facility's cryogenic repository of experimental white mice, Hank starts with his experiments to improve the black box technology. However, this leads to several failures as the signal's increased strength kills the test subjects too quickly to get meaningful results, but Hank resolves to continue anyway, making multiple trips to and from the cryonic repository. Eventually, though, his experimental progress stalls due to the mice dying too quickly, so he explores more of the complex. He ultimately finds his way to the "Premium Elite Plus" suite where Hank thaws out one of them, a man named [[Stephen Winthrop]]. The confused Steve is attached with a black box chip by Hank as he wonders if he was brought out because of Reclamation Day; Hank claims that it is, but not as Vault-Tec planned.

As Hank prepares Steve for his current experiment, he remarks about his preference for "the classics" and how much has changed in the world. Steve becomes more aware of his helpless state as Hank reveals that he did not include his family in the program, which Steve claims was because it was too expensive to buy more than one ticket. Hank emphasizes how a commitment to family builds a civilization, then compares it to his experiments, claiming that through enhancing the black box, he will also improve a piece of "outdated technology" — civilization. Steve begins to react to the black box's signal as Hank increases its strength, eventually resulting in his explosive death; Hank coldly remarks "if at first you don't succeed... try, try again."

===[[The Wrangler]]===
Now with his experiments transitioning into human testing, Hank decides to "recruit" from outside the facility. His search brings him to an [[abandoned taco stand]] where he finds the [[snake oil salesman]] on a "date" with [[Fisto]], wherein he knocks the salesman out with a crowbar and transports him back to the facility. The salesman later awakens to find himself in Hank's workstation, restrained to a table with a black box chip implanted in his neck. Hank asks if he would like to forget every memory he has ever had, including people he had met and details about his life, while retaining the use of his language and motor skills; the salesman eagerly agrees. Hank then uses a machine to remove the memories from his brain. At first, the salesman seems pleased and even aroused but then begins to wince seemingly in pain and scream as Hank turns up the dial. Unlike with Stephen Winthrop, the salesman's head does not explode, and the procedure proves to be a success. As he looks over the docile salesman, Hank relishes the results of his experiments finally achieving progress and presses a button on his console to begin the next stage of his plan.

At some point during his tests, Hank learned that Lucy had made it to New Vegas to hunt him down. He dispatched the mind-controlled salesman as his representative to strike a deal with the Ghoul to have his daughter returned to Vault 33 while he completed his plans. Evidently suspecting that something would go awry, Hank came to the surface in person, finding Lucy collapsed on the floor of her room in the [[Atomic Wrangler Casino]] shortly after she launched the Ghoul out the window for betraying her. He comforts his daughter as she falls unconscious from a tranquilizer and later transports her to the facility.

===[[The Other Player]]===
In the following morning, Lucy wakes up inside a room of the facility which has been made to resemble a domestic bedroom. Sneaking outside, she notices two [[Legionary (TV series)|legionaries]] walking down a hall towards her room, and attempts to ambush them, but is taken aback when they greet her cheerfully as if nothing is wrong.

Continuing to investigate, Lucy discovers the room with many desks, which is now occupied many [[X-Branch office worker|office workers]] in matching attire, who barely acknowledge Lucy's presence and simply work cheerfully among themselves to produce more of the black box chips. Lucy continues further past and finally finds her father preparing food in a simulation room dressed up as a kitchen and dining room. Though Lucy is on her guard, Hank seems completely unfazed to see her, and attempts to win her over to his side by deriding the seeming pointlessness of the factional violence on the surface. Unconvinced, Lucy threatens Hank with a pair of scissors and tells him she is taking him back to Vault 33 to face justice; Hank handcuffs himself and seemingly surrenders, leaving with Lucy.

When the two return to the room with many desks, the office workers are shocked to see Hank being threatened, and all eyes turn on Hank and Lucy. Hank reassures them that everything is fine; he claims to Lucy that he has been using the black box chips to give new, peaceful lives to people on the surface who were formerly murderers or otherwise locked in violent conflict. Lucy does not buy his argument that this is benevolent and tries to tell the brainwashed workers they are free, but they refuse to leave, believing the facility is safer for them than the surface. Hank suggests that he and Lucy will simply leave the entrances to the facility open once they leave so that the workers can make their own choice on whether to stay. As Lucy prepares to take him away, Hank instead has her remain there as he opens up a door leading to an elevator, which he calls a "management perk."

Just then, the brainwashed snake oil salesman and another worker wheel in [[Biff (TV series)|Ranger Biff]] and a [[massive legionary]], both of whom have been captured and tied to chairs. They have been implanted with black box chips, but the chips have not yet been activated, and the salesman asks Hank for permission. Hank defers to Lucy, and she refuses, telling them to let the two men go. However, the massive legionary is untied first and immediately begins trying to attack Biff for being a member of the NCR. Lucy tries to protect Biff but is ultimately unable to stop the legionary from repeatedly attacking Biff with a stapler. With no one else intervening, and Hank presenting the black box chips as the only way to save Biff, Lucy is convinced to activate the two men's chips, immediately rendering them docile and friendly towards each other as they leave to treat Biff's wounds.

===[[The Handoff]]===
Reflecting on the scene that just occurred and noticing the snake oil salesman standing by blankly, Lucy asks Hank about the black box chips, which he states are configured to give people selective degrees of amnesia, which can then be replaced with preset memories. Lucy continues to view this as wrong and asks Hank how she can stop him; he claims that the people who have already been chipped cannot be returned to their original state, but that Lucy can prevent him from creating more chips if she destroys the mainframe that controls all of the chips. He leads her towards the room with the mainframe, allowing her to drive the golf cart.

Just outside the door to the mainframe room, Lucy is distracted from her goal by a worker behind them who says that the floor is wet due to his mopping; Lucy realizes that this worker was the massive legionary, now rendered completely harmless. This causes her to falter and become swayed by Hank's arguments, believing that the chips can be used to pacify Caesar's Legion and prevent them from harming anyone else in the Mojave.

Hank, continuing to try and sway Lucy to his side, has the two of them eat mashed potatoes in the simulation dining room. Lucy seems to be fine at first, but when she sees that Biff is serving them, now no longer remembering anything about his past life with the NCR or even having previously met Lucy a week ago, she turns against Hank for good. Hank dismisses Biff and tells Lucy his opinion that the NCR and the Legion are equally problematic, pointing to the NCR's taxes and imperialism before burning his hand when taking a pie out of the oven. Lucy, still feigning support, pretends to be freeing Hank from his handcuffs, but instead cuffs him to the oven, revealing that she is aware he was manipulating her the whole time. She leaves to destroy the mainframe while Hank loses his cool.

Lucy drives the golf cart, shakily, down the halls towards the mainframe room, passing by the comms room but failing to hear her brother [[Norm MacLean|Norm]] transmit his own message asking Lucy for help and calling out their father for hiding the true nature of Vault 33 from them. She opens the mainframe room, crowbar in hand, but is shocked to see that the mainframe is actually a severed head (that of pre-War [[United States Congress|U.S. Congresswoman]] [[Diane Welch]], though Lucy does not know this) attached to a neural interface helmet.

==Behind the scenes==
* {{Note|(Note 1)}} This is a descriptive title, as no official name for the facility has been confirmed, though the Ghoul calls it a special "Vault for management."<ref name="management" /> Some characters later seen inside the facility are credited as "X-Branch office workers."<ref name="xbranch">{{FOTV|2|6}}: Two of the mind-controlled wastelanders seen in the facility are credited as being "X-Branch office workers."</ref> In behind the scenes materials and interviews, the facility (or at least the parts of it seen in the show) has been called the "X-Branch" or "Site X."<ref group="Meta" name="vfx">[https://archive.ph/HGQ3K Art of VFX: "Fallout – Season 2: Vincent Poitras – VFX Supervisor – Raynault VFX"]</ref>
* According to lead VFX artist Jay Worth, the exterior of the tower structure seen inside the Vault was filmed at the Wrapper Building in Culver City. He stated he and his team chose the building because of its unique structure, then added additional details in post-production, including pipes and bridges.<ref group="Meta" name="vfx" /><ref group="Meta">[https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/the-wrapper-los-angeles-california Atlas Obscura: The (W)rapper, Los Angeles, California]</ref>

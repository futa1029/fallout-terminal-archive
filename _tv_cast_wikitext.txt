'''''Fallout''''' is a post-apocalyptic drama television series based on the [[Fallout series|video game series]] of the same name. While sharing the [[Fallout setting|setting]] of the franchise's mainline video games, the plot is that of an original story rather than attempting to retell one from previous entries.<ref name="GoddHoward" /> Licensed to Amazon Studios,<ref name="Kilter" /> the series was developed by [[Kilter Films]], in association with [[Bethesda Softworks|Bethesda Game Studios]] and [[Bethesda Softworks]]. Executive producers include [[Jonathan Nolan]], [[Lisa Joy]], and [[Athena Wickham]] for Kilter Films, [[Todd Howard]] for Bethesda Game Studios, and [[James Altman]] for Bethesda Softworks.<ref name="Deadline">[https://web.archive.org/web/20210228001003/https://deadline.com/2020/07/fallout-television-series-based-game-franchise-amazon-studios-westworld-creators-1202976079/ Deadline Hollywood: ‘Fallout’ TV Series From ‘Westworld’ Creators Based On Games In Works At Amazon With Series Commitment (archived)]</ref>

''Fallout'' entered production in mid-2022, with Jonathan Nolan directing the first three episodes,<ref name="Deadline2" /> as well as [[Geneva Robertson-Dworet]] and [[Graham Wagner]] serving as showrunners.<ref name="showrun">[https://deadline.com/2022/01/fallout-series-games-prime-video-jonathan-nolan-geneva-robertson-dworet-graham-wagner-kilter-1234905676/ ‘Fallout’: Kilter Films’ TV Series Based On Games Moving Forward At Prime Video With Jonathan Nolan Directing; Duo Set As Showrunners - Deadline]</ref> Production and filming for the first season took place across New York, New Jersey, Utah and the ghost town of Kolmanskop in the country of Namibia.<ref>[https://web.archive.org/web/20220430203810/https://stuntaccess.com/coordinator/job/fallout-w-t-hondo-tv-series-new-york/ Fallout (w/t: "Hondo") – TV Series – New York, New Jersey, Utah - Stunt Access (archived)]</ref><ref>[https://ageofthenerd.com/2024/04/exploring-the-fallout-universe-an-interview-with-jonathan-nolan-and-michael-emerson "Exploring the Fallout Universe: An Interview with Jonathan Nolan and Michael Emerson" - Age of the Nerd]:<br />[[Jonathan Nolan]]: ''"I mean you're always worried that with an adaptation there there are things that you can't do. We knew that we couldn't beat Bethesda when it came to computer graphics, so we went to the old game plan, the playbook that I've always played by growing up with my brother, we just shot practically in amazing places, we picked ourselves up, packed an overnight bag, went down to Namibia, went out to the western salt flats of Utah, and found some natural splendor and beauty, and brought our amazing cast and crew with us."''<br />[[Michael Emerson]]: ''"That set in the Nevada desert with the downed Soviet satellite, it was, when I walked out there and saw that we were going to be performing in the shadow of this amazing prop, God..."''<br />Jonathan Nolan: ''"That's Howard Cummings, our production designer, is just, just incredible."''</ref>

The series premiered on April 10, 2024. It was renewed for a [[Fallout TV series season 2|second season]],<ref name="s2twitter"/> which began filming in November 2024 in Los Angeles<ref name="s2filmingnov"/><ref name="s2filmstart"/> under the codename ''Dakota'', and entered post-production circa May 8, 2025.<ref name="filming end">[https://archive.ph/rtGxm ‘Fallout’ Season 2 Gets a Nuclear Update From Ella Purnell - Collider (archived)]</ref> On May 12, 2025, it was announced that season 2 would release in December 2025, and that the series had also been renewed for a [[Fallout TV series season 3|third season]].<ref>[https://archive.ph/L2Tyi Amazon: "‘Fallout’ renewed for Season 3 ahead of Season 2 premiere on Prime Video" - Amazon (archived)]</ref> Season 2 premiered on December 16, 2025, and released eight episodes weekly, culminating in a season finale on February 3, 2026.

The ''Fallout'' TV series is not to be confused with ''[[Fallout|Fallout: A Post Nuclear Role Playing Game]]'' (identified on this wiki as ''Fallout'' for short), the 1997 first entry of the ''Fallout'' franchise. Despite belonging to separate mediums, both installments are set in the same region of post-apocalyptic California, with the town of [[Shady Sands]] appearing in both media.

==Plot==
{{Quotation|Based on one of the greatest video game series of all time, Fallout is the story of haves and have-nots in a world in which there’s almost nothing left to have. 200 years after the apocalypse, the gentle denizens of luxury fallout shelters are forced to return to the irradiated hellscape their ancestors left behind — and are shocked to discover an incredibly complex, gleefully weird and highly violent universe waiting for them.|[[:File:Fallout - Teaser Trailer - Prime Video|Description, ''Fallout - Teaser Trailer - Prime Video'']]}}

[[File:LA being nuked.gif|thumb|200px|left|Nukes dropped on Los Angeles in 2077 on the day of the [[Great War]]]]

The story of ''Fallout'' is set in [[Timeline#2296|2296]],{{Ref|(Note 1)}} 219 years after the global [[Nuclear weapons|nuclear]] holocaust known as the [[Great War]]. During this apocalyptic event, some managed to survive in [[Vault]]s, massive fallout shelters constructed by the [[Vault-Tec Corporation]] across the [[United States of America|United States]], while leaving the rest outside to suffer and struggle for survival in the [[wasteland]].

[[File:Lucybts.jpg|thumb|200px|left|[[Lucy MacLean]], one protagonist]]

[[Lucy MacLean]] is a resident of one such Vault: [[Vault 33]], located in the ruined city of [[Boneyard|Los Angeles]]. She has spent her life underground and is currently applying to have a husband from [[Vault 32]] in an arranged marriage. After a tragic event involving a woman named [[Lee Moldaver]], Lucy decides to leave for the surface on a mission to save her father [[Hank MacLean|Hank]].<ref name="S1desc">[https://web.archive.org/web/20240314103018/https://press.amazonstudios.com/us/en/original-series/fallout/1 ''Fallout'' Season 1 description on Amazon Studios (archived)]</ref>

[[File:Maximus TV series.png|thumb|200px|left|[[Maximus]], another protagonist]]

As she becomes accustomed to the dangers of the wasteland, she encounters other characters including [[Maximus]], a young soldier in the [[Brotherhood of Steel]], as well as [[the Ghoul]], a [[ghoul]]ified [[bounty hunter]].<ref name="VF1"/> The protagonists' paths converge as they chase an artifact from an enigmatic researcher named [[Siggi Wilzig]] which has the potential to reshape the power dynamic of the entire wasteland.<ref name="S1desc"/> Other perspectives include that of pre-War Hollywood star, Cooper Howard, the man who became the Ghoul; as well as the continued story of Vault 33's dwellers after Lucy leaves on her journey, shown through the perspective of her brother [[Norm MacLean]].<ref name="WastelandInterview">[https://web.archive.org/web/20240403200010/https://www.ign.com/articles/fallout-tv-series-amazon-prime-video-cast-showrunners-interview-wasteland-factions-vault-ghoul IGN: "Fallout: A Wasteland Survival Guide to the New TV Series" (archived)]</ref>

Other details about the series include [[Filly]], a junkyard-like settlement in the greater Los Angeles area; the ''[[The Prydwen|Prydwen]]'', an airship built and used by the Brotherhood; as well as [[Vault-Tec Corporation]] mascot [[Vault Boy]] being given a backstory.<ref name="VF1" /> The series features four Vaults ([[Vault 4|4]], [[Vault 31|31]], 32, and 33), the latter three are interconnected via exchanging resources.<ref name="IGN CCXP">[https://web.archive.org/web/20231210115513/https://www.ign.com/articles/the-big-fallout-tv-show-interview-with-jonathan-nolan-walton-goggins-and-more The Big Fallout TV Show Interview With Jonathan Nolan, Walton Goggins, and More - IGN (archived)]</ref>

===Themes===
{{Quotation|One of the things that Ella's character gets to experience when she comes to the surface is expecting the world to be empty and the world is very full and very rich, but it's still filled with division tribalism. Even if you destroyed humanity, there's enough of us left to keep those divisions going. We're squabbling now over nothing. So it's one of the richer questions that we get to play with.|Jonathan Nolan|[https://web.archive.org/web/20231210115513/https://www.ign.com/articles/the-big-fallout-tv-show-interview-with-jonathan-nolan-walton-goggins-and-more IGN (archived)]}}
Similar to the video games, ''Fallout'' features sociopolitical commentary and was intended to challenge the idea of black-and-white morality. A major goal was to get away from the binary thinking of people as being simply good or evil.<ref name="VF1"/>

[[File:Vault33sublevel4two.jpg|thumb|250px|Vault Dwellers having a supply of food]]

One of the story's themes is about exploring America as a place of haves and have-nots, and how being wealthy and never having to struggle for basic amenities such as food can drastically alter someone's perspective and opinions. ''Fallout'' explores Lucy's collision with the hard reality of other people's experiences and what happened to the people who had no Vault and were left behind to die. Director Nolan compares this to real-life America, saying the wealth gap division has intensified over the last decades. Lucy's status as a Vault Dweller is mocked by characters such as [[Ma June]] and the Ghoul who view her as privileged.

Another intended theme regarding the Vaults is them being used as a metaphor about the hardships of immigration, particularly countries with open/closed border policies, and what excluding certain people could mean.<ref name="moviefone">[https://web.archive.org/web/20240307175615/https://www.moviefone.com/news/10-things-we-learned-at-the-fallout-press-conference/ Moviefone: 10 Things We Learned at the ‘Fallout’ Press Conference with Cast and Crew (archived)]</ref> Other themes include the morality of the death penalty, how people turn to religion in times of grief, how civilizations and factions are not immortal, how people's individual freedoms and human rights may be under attack, and the importance of exercising caution when dealing with strangers. Another theme is how people may not be what they seem, with Lucy discovering a major revelation about someone she trusted, while also jumping to negative conclusions about a particular group who seem evil and sinister, but she winds up misjudging their intentions.

===Canonicity===
According to [[Todd Howard]], the events of the ''Fallout'' TV series are considered part of the official ''Fallout'' canon, as he states, "We view what's happening in the show as canon." [[Bethesda Softworks]] has had some amount of oversight to ensure that the scripts fit within the existing world of ''Fallout'' and the series' previous titles.<ref name="VF1" /> Howard and other show creators view the TV series as a mainline series entry in its own right, akin to ''[[Fallout 5]]''.<ref>[https://web.archive.org/web/20231209185430/https://www.ign.com/articles/fallout-show-creators-envision-the-prime-video-series-as-a-fallout-5 Fallout Show Creators Envision the Prime Video Series as a ‘Fallout 5’ - IGN (srchived)]</ref>

'''The Whitespring presidential cottage and museum''', also known as the '''Appalachian White House''',<ref>[[The Whitespring presidential cottage terminal entries#STAFF MENU|The Whitespring presidential cottage terminal entries; presidential cottage & museum terminal, STAFF MENU]]</ref> is an unmarked [[Fallout 76 locations|location]] within [[the Whitespring]] in the [[Savage Divide]] {{in|FO76}}.

==Background==
This two-story structure was once organized as a museum tourist attraction, with each of the rooms furnished to emphasize its grandeur and past splendor,<ref group="Non-game">''[[Fallout 76 Vault Dweller's Survival Guide]]'' pp. 425–426: ''"THE WHITESPRING GOLF CLUB''<br />''"This is part of The Whitespring Resort, and the exterior map is referenced with that location. Looking amazingly untouched by the ravages of war, this golf club (once popular with politicians and titans of industry) has been kept well manicured by robotic attendants, as the human occupants are looking distinctly emaciated. There's a full 18 holes to explore (with small stone storage huts and a refreshment kiosk to gather loot from), though the main area of interest is the club house (for junk appropriation). The row of white bungalows and Presidential Cottage and Museum along the edge of the location are off-limits unless you have the necessary hand scanner code."''<br />([[Fallout 76 Vault Dweller's Survival Guide Atlas of Appalachia]])</ref> such as the state dining room that has hosted more than a dozen formal banquets with heads of state. Furniture was American-made, most dating to the eighteenth century to maintain authenticity, with the most notable piece being the mahogany grand cabinet, which previously displayed The Whitespring's Presidential Platinum China, and a display cabinet with a saber carried by [[Ulysses S. Grant|President Ulysses S. Grant]] during the Vicksburg Campaign of the [[American Civil War]]. His iconic saber was lost at the Battle of Champion Hill, and was recovered the following day.<ref>[[The Whitespring presidential cottage terminal entries#State Dining Room|The Whitespring presidential cottage terminal entries; presidential cottage & museum terminal, state dining room]]</ref>

The Hall of Heroes displayed a collection of high-quality reproduction oil paintings commemorating heroes of the [[Revolutionary War|American Revolutionary War]], leading directly to the president's portico on the rear of the house, with a  view of the [[Springhill golf course]]. The deck chairs on the portico were purchased from the [[White House]] in [[Timeline#2042|2042]] following renovations to the White House pool.<ref>[[The Whitespring presidential cottage terminal entries#Hall of Heroes|The Whitespring presidential cottage terminal entries; presidential cottage & museum terminal, Hall of Heroes]]</ref> The final room on the ground floor is the social parlor, providing a comfortable space for casual discussions and evening entertainment, such as after formal dinners, with the grand piano in the room having been played by several notable pianists and innumerable presidential children. The cello, though once believed to be an original Stradivarius, was later determined to be a forgery, yet was retained as an item of potential interest to visitors.<ref>[[The Whitespring presidential cottage terminal entries#Social Parlor|The Whitespring presidential cottage terminal entries; presidential cottage & museum terminal, social parlor]]</ref>

The presidential suite on the top floor provided privacy away from the other guests of the resort, with the final exposition before the [[Great War]] incorporating an antique television and phone from the Roosevelt White House, as well as a bureau that once belonged to [[Millard Fillmore|President Millard Fillmore]]. Although the bed was originally a traditional four-poster, the 1887 mahogany bed was refashioned into a more modern design following damage during remodeling in [[Timeline#2054|2054]].<ref>[[The Whitespring presidential cottage terminal entries#Presidential Suite|The Whitespring presidential cottage terminal entries; presidential cottage & museum terminal, presidential suite]]</ref> The other room was the children's suite, for members of the president's family, with toys included on the exposition having been donated by each of the children who have stayed in the cottage, representing different eras in American history, from an original 1903 [[Teddy bear (Fallout 76)|teddy bear]], through icons of the mid-century space race, to the modern [[Giddyup Buttercup (Fallout 76)|Giddyup Buttercup]].<ref>[[The Whitespring presidential cottage terminal entries#Children's Suite|The Whitespring presidential cottage terminal entries; presidential cottage & museum terminal, children's suite]]</ref>

==Layout==
The cottage is a part of the world space and has two floors, with high-level white collar loot, including a case containing [[Grant's saber]], the objective of the [[Order of Mysteries]] quest [[Forging a Legend]]. Gaining access to this case requires collecting The Whitespring staff password from a corpse upstairs, under the shower within the children's suite. A skeleton surrounded by chems lays over the side of the bed in the presidential suite, leaning over a basket.

==Notable loot==
* The [[presidential cottage security recording]], a holotape contained in the presidential cottage and museum terminal, found in the staff access section.
* The [[Fallout 76 keys#Presidential cottage password|presidential cottage passwords]] can be found in two locations: in the children's suite upstairs shower room, and on the broken [[Whitespring protectron#Whitespring docent|Whitespring docent]] protectron.
* [[Grant's saber]] - On the first floor, in the case
* A [[Stealth Boy (Fallout 76)|Stealth Boy]] can be found in the children's suite upstairs shower room, next to a skeleton.
